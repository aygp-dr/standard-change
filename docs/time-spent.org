#+TITLE: What the invariants actually cost
#+DATE: 2026-09-13

* Why measure this

The repo argues, repeatedly, that certain invariants are worth their cost. This
is the cost, measured rather than asserted: *105 commits to =main= between
06:19 and 14:41 local*, one working day, classified by which invariant the
commit was serving.

Commit counts are a proxy and a poor one — a one-line fix and a subsystem both
count once. Treat the *shape* as the finding, not the arithmetic.

* The distribution

| invariant                        | commits | share | first | last  |
|----------------------------------+---------+-------+-------+-------|
| one writer per fact              |      20 |   19% | 08:46 | 13:58 |
| observation is not intent        |      12 |   11% | 08:00 | 14:08 |
| reserve is not deploy            |       8 |    8% | 06:36 | 14:06 |
| the merge is settlement          |       7 |    7% | 06:19 | 13:57 |
| a gate that cannot fail          |       3 |    3% | 07:44 | 14:41 |
| unreachable is not falsified     |       2 |    2% | 08:22 | 14:34 |
| blast radius is declared         |       2 |    2% | 06:22 | 10:36 |
| the oracle must be independent   |       1 |    1% | 09:18 | 09:18 |
| everything else                  |      50 |   48% |       |       |

* What the numbers say, and what they do not

** The expensive one is naming, not mechanism

*"One writer per fact" cost the most — 19%, five hours* — and almost none of it
was building anything. It was:

- =staging:passed= naming no environment, so a pass from one estate satisfied a
  guard about another
- =production:healthy= flipping five times between a person and a workflow
- =change:*= holding both classification and lifecycle, so "a PR has at most one
  =change:*=" could not be stated
- renaming to =itil:*= across nine files and sixteen live labels
- =hold:staging= being rejected once =freeze= and precedence existed

*Every one was a name that meant two things.* The mechanism was rarely wrong;
the vocabulary was, and the vocabulary is what every guard branches on.

** The cheap-looking ones are cheap because they fired early

"Blast radius is declared" cost 2% and stopped at 10:36 — not because it
stopped mattering, but because =shared/**= marking every app was settled once
and then simply worked. Same for "the oracle must be independent": one commit,
09:18, and it has been load-bearing ever since.

*A low number here means the invariant was decided once and held.* That is the
best possible outcome and it is indistinguishable, in this table, from an
invariant nobody worked on.

** The ones that span the whole day are the ones still not finished

"Observation is not intent" runs 08:00 to 14:08. "Unreachable is not falsified"
runs 08:22 to 14:34 on *two* commits — one in the morning, one six hours later,
because the same defect reappeared in a different place: first
=merge-on-healthy= recording unreachable-as-unhealthy, then =preflight= reading
nineteen never-started check runs as red.

*A wide span with few commits is a defect class, not a task.* It keeps coming
back somewhere new.

** Half the work is not classifiable as an invariant at all

48% is apps, docs, deployment mechanics, and repairs to things I had broken an
hour earlier. That is the honest denominator: *the invariants cost about half a
day out of a day*, and the other half was the ordinary work they were protecting.

* The cost nobody counts

None of the above includes the time spent *finding* the defects, which was
mostly not spent committing. Of the sixteen issues filed today, the expensive
ones were found by:

- running a deployment and reading the output (guard 5 on =deadbee=)
- a subagent reading a diff (=mktemp -t=, which would have silently reduced e2e
  from 27 checks to 0 on CI)
- an agent doing something unrelated (the XSS, found while editing OneUI for a
  footer)
- *thirty minutes chasing a CI/local divergence that did not exist*, because
  GitHub had stopped scheduling jobs and a never-started check reports the same
  =failure= as a real one

That last is the sharpest number in this document. *One defect class —
unreachable collapsed into false — cost more wall-clock time in a single
episode than most invariants cost all day*, and it cost it by making a correct
system look broken.

* What I would tell someone starting this

1. *Spend the time on names first.* It was the largest cost and the one that
   propagated — every guard reads the vocabulary, so an ambiguous label is a
   bug in everything downstream of it.
2. *Budget for the same defect arriving three times.* Unreachable-is-not-false
   appeared in the merge workflow, in a marker script, and in preflight. Fixing
   it once taught nothing to the other two.
3. *The invariants are not the expensive part.* Half the day was ordinary work.
   The invariants made that half safe; they did not replace it.
