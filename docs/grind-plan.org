#+TITLE: Two rounds of ten, and what each is for
#+DATE: 2026-09-13

* Round one — the positive path, ten times

Ten small real changes, one at a time, through the whole flow: book, preflight,
staging, observe, guard 4, idle colour, cutover, guard 5, settle.

*Measured*: one complete cycle is *71 seconds* — 27s to staging-green, 44s
through production and settle. Eight more is about ten minutes of machine time.

*What it is for*: the happy path is the only path that has ever run. Ten
repetitions say whether it is stable or whether run 1 was luck.

*What it already found*, in the first two runs alone:
- =observe.sh= wrote the label but not the record, so guard 4 printed "label
  present" and "no observation recorded" two lines apart
- the record it did write used an instrument name matching nothing
- =release:start= survived settlement
- every branch would have *reverted* two control-plane files, including an
  emergency-bypass fix committed twenty minutes earlier
- a repurposed branch carried a stray hunk in an app its title never mentioned,
  which would have deployed that app on the authority of a change about another

*Expected yield from runs 3–10*: low, and that is the point. If they all pass
identically the happy path is stable. A finding late in the run is worth more
than the same finding early, because it means the defect is intermittent.

* Round two — freezes and emergencies, ten times

The same ten changes, interrupted. *This is the untested half* — every cleanup
in this pipeline runs only on the success path (=docs/adr/0001=, "we have only
ever walked the positive case", issue #37).

** The sequence to practise

For each run, interrupt at a different point and then recover:

| # | interrupt at        | with                        | then                       |
|---+---------------------+-----------------------------+----------------------------|
| 1 | before booking      | =freeze=                    | clear, re-book             |
| 2 | after booking       | =freeze=                    | clear, resume              |
| 3 | before staging      | =emergency= on the estate   | clear, restart             |
| 4 | mid-staging         | =freeze=                    | clear, re-run the gates    |
| 5 | after staging green | =emergency=                 | clear, revalidate          |
| 6 | before cutover      | =freeze=                    | clear, re-verify the idle colour |
| 7 | after cutover       | =emergency=                 | settle or abort?           |
| 8 | at settlement       | =freeze=                    | does settle complete?      |
| 9 | an =itil:emergency= change *during* a freeze | — | it should proceed |
| 10 | two emergencies at once | —                       | what orders them?          |

** The questions each one answers

Every row is a state nobody has produced. The ones I expect to hurt:

- *#7* — production is already serving the change when the estate closes. There
  is no verb for "stop, but it is already out there". =switch.sh= can roll back
  and nothing decides whether it should.
- *#8* — =settle.sh= is not reentrant and has no abort. It died mid-run once
  today on an undefined variable and left a change merged with labels
  uncleared; a freeze arriving at the same point is the same shape with a
  cause.
- *#10* — nothing orders two emergencies. =itil:emergency= grants an exemption
  from the estate's blockers, so two of them exempt each other and the berth
  decides, arbitrarily.
- *F5 from PR #48* — an emergency waiting on a berth held by a change that is
  waiting on the emergency. A deadlock with nothing to release it.

** What round two is really testing

Not whether the guards refuse — they do, and round one shows it. *Whether the
estate can be put back.* A pipeline that stops safely and cannot resume is a
pipeline that turns every interruption into an incident.

** The round-two rows as named checks

Audited 2026-09-13 against =sim/label_sim.py= on =origin/feat/label-simulator=
(PR #48). The freeze and emergency *moves* are all in the successor relation --
=human/freeze= / =human/thaw=, =human/itil:emergency= / =human/withdraw=, and
under the =estate-emergency= repair the bare =emergency= toggle -- so every row
below is a state the sweep can construct. What is missing is not reachability.
It is that five rows have nothing that *checks* them, and three cannot be asked
at all because the move that would answer them does not exist.

These are the identifiers to regrind against. R-numbers are the round-two rows;
they are NOT the F-numbers from PR #48, which are findings, not checks, and
exist only in that PR's body.

| id  | row                          | reachable | checked by                     |
|-----+------------------------------+-----------+--------------------------------|
| R1  | freeze before booking        | yes       | -- (preflight returns 4 first; the freeze is never the reason) |
| R2  | freeze after booking         | yes       | =inv_ordinary_change_waits= (refusal only) |
| R3  | estate emergency pre-staging | yes       | =inv_ordinary_change_waits=    |
| R4  | freeze mid-staging           | state yes | *nothing* -- see below         |
| R5  | emergency after staging green| yes       | =inv_ordinary_change_waits=    |
| R6  | freeze before cutover        | yes       | =inv_ordinary_change_waits=    |
| R7  | emergency after cutover      | *no move* | --                             |
| R8  | freeze at settlement         | yes       | *nothing*                      |
| R9  | =itil:emergency= during freeze | yes     | =inv_emergency_never_waits=    |
| R10 | two emergencies at once      | yes       | *excluded by comment*          |

*** R4 -- the central law is honoured at one site and nowhere else

=successors= re-checks =preflight= at the production move:

: if rules.guard4(st, i)[0] == 0 and rules.preflight(st, i)[0] == 0:

That is the law ("a guard whose subject can change after it is checked must be
re-checked at the moment it is relied on") and it is why R5 and R6 come for
free. But once =berth=True=, the three instruments -- =gates/e2e.sh=,
=smoke.sh=, =human/observe.sh uat= -- fire with no guard between them. A freeze
arriving mid-staging changes nothing in the model, and nothing in the model
says it should. Same shape as guard 4b, one rung lower.

*** R7 -- there is no verb for "it is already out there"

=inprod= is set True by the production move and cleared only by
=author/push=. No rollback, no abort. The state "production is serving the
change and the estate has just closed" is reachable; the question the row asks
cannot be phrased.

*** R8 -- and the berth is stuck behind it

=may_merge= never consults =preflight=, so a freeze landing between
=change:complete= and the merge does not stop the merge. If it did, the berth is
released only *by* the merge, so the change would sit holding staging with
nothing to release it -- and =sweep= is safety-only. There is no liveness
property anywhere in the file: nothing asks whether a good terminal state is
still reachable from a state the search has found. That is exactly what round
two is for, and the simulator cannot currently ask it.

*** R10 -- the checker declines the case

From =inv_emergency_never_waits=:

: if "E" in pr_get(st.prs[h], "cls"):
:     continue                      # two emergencies is a different case

The one row flagged as "nothing orders them" is the one row the checker skips.

*** What round two needs added to the simulator

Three moves and one property class, none of which are prerequisites for running
round two by hand -- they are what makes it repeatable afterwards:

- =targets/switch.sh rollback= : clears =inprod=, so R7 has an answer
- =change/settle.sh --abort= : releases the berth without merging, so R8 has one
- window *expiry* : today =change:scheduled= never lapses, so R1's re-book and
  "we blew past our window" are the same state
- a liveness sweep : from every reachable state, is a merged-or-abandoned
  terminal state still reachable? This is the *whole* question -- not whether
  the guards refuse, but whether the estate can be put back.

** The state with no name: in production, out of window, not merged

Observed 2026-09-13 on #38, and it is R7 and R8 arriving together.

A change deploys to staging inside its window, spends the window being
measured, and the window closes while it is still mid-flight. If it had reached
production first, the estate is now *serving a build that is not on =main=*, and
the change request that authorized it has expired. Three facts, all true at
once:

- production serves the change
- no open window covers it
- =main= does not contain it

Nothing in the pipeline can say that. =change:complete= is the only terminal
state and it is set by =settle.sh=, which merges. There was no verb for "it is
out there and we are not finishing".

*** Why it is worse than it sounds

The next change to come along reads =main= as the truth about production. It
branches from a =main= that lacks the deployed change, passes every gate against
that base, and promoting it *silently reverts* the earlier change -- not as a
rollback anybody decided on, but as a side effect of the branch point. That is
the =pipeline= divergence class in =change/divergence.sh=, except the diverging
artifact is production itself.

The blue/green front makes it quieter still: the idle colour holds the older
build, so a rollback "works", and what it rolls back to is the state everyone's
=main= agrees on. The estate heals itself into having lost the change.

*** What we do about it now

=change/abort.sh= gives the state a name and a closure code:

- =change:failed= -- production never served it. Nothing to undo.
- =change:backed-out= -- production served it and was returned. =--to <sha>= is
  *required*, because the word asserts an estate action and a closure naming
  none is the forged-evidence defect this repo keeps finding.

Neither merges. Both clear the berth, redline the window, withdraw the
deployment records and clear the observation labels, and both post the record
*before* clearing -- the labels are working state, the comment is the record.

=change/uat.sh= stops the state being reachable by accident: it re-reads the
window before the smoke walk and again after it, so an acceptance that
straddles the close is refused rather than recorded.

*** Still not fixed

=change/schedule.sh current= returns *exit 0 for an expired window*. It finds
the PR's window and never compares the end to now, which is why =preflight=
said NOT ON THE CALENDAR for #38 at 20:39 while =current= happily handed back a
window that closed at 20:30. =activate.sh= uses =current=. Two readers of one
calendar, disagreeing about the one thing a calendar is for. =uat.sh= works
around it by reading the end itself; the divergence is the real defect and this
is a second implementation, not a fix.
