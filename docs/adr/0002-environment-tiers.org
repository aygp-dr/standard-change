#+TITLE: ADR 0002 — protected and team environments
#+DATE: 2026-09-13
#+OPTIONS: toc:2 num:nil

* Status

*Superseded by ADR 0001* (2026-09-13), which was retitled to
/automatic promotion to production, gated by evidence/.

This was filed as a separate decision about environments and dashboards. It is
not one. If promotion is automatic, *which environments can be promoted into*
is a safety property of that decision, and what is worth watching follows from
it. Splitting them into two ADRs implied two choices where there was one.

Kept for the reasoning and the MonoWatch evaluation; the decision itself now
lives in ADR 0001 §Consequence — the environment tiers.

* Context

Two questions turned out to be the same question.

*What environments exist?* =README.org= has said since early on that team
environments are for developing a change while staging is the path to
production, and that a team environment can never promote. That distinction
lived in prose and in nobody's head.

*What is worth watching?* Evaluating MonoWatch made the gap concrete. It renders
17 services × 3 environments, and in a monorepo *every cell shows the same
build* — a 51-cell grid displaying one fact. Its System Configuration panel is
per-service enable/disable toggles, which presumes services with independent
lifecycles.

*MonoWatch is not wrong; it is a dashboard for a different architecture* — the
one ADR 0001 options A and A2 would move us to. Using it now would render an
architecture we do not have.

* Decision

*Two tiers, and the port number says which.*

| tier          | ports       | promotes? | watched? |
|---------------+-------------+-----------+----------|
| *dev*         | 9000–9099   | no        | *no* — disposable |
| *team*        | 9100–9199   | *no*      | listed, not monitored |
| *protected*   | 9200–9299   | *yes*     | *yes* — this is the estate |

A tier is a property of the *address*, not a rule someone remembers: anything
at 9100–9199 is a dead end by construction, and =ports.sh= refuses a dev block
that would cross into it.

** The dashboard follows from the tiers

=./dashboard= shows four panels, in this order:

1. *change schedule / queue* — the berth is the scarce thing, so it is first
2. *protected* — staging, then blue and green with one marked ACTIVE and the
   other named as the rollback target
3. *team* — listed, so the tier is visible, marked not provisioned
4. *open changes* — with their labels, because the labels are the state

Dev is *absent on purpose*. Six lines of estate state, against MonoWatch's 51
cells, is the honest amount a one-berth monorepo has.

*What we do not show, and why:* DORA metrics. =research/history/release-standards.org=
established that every DORA metric takes a deployment that reached production
as its unit, so a forfeited window and a revalidation are invisible to it — and
those are our dominant cost. A DORA row here would read =Deploy Freq: 0/day,
Failure Rate: 0%= while the pipeline burns 10–20 validation runs per shipped
change. *Displaying a metric that cannot see the cost is worse than displaying
nothing.*

* What we are not building yet

The team tier is *specified and unprovisioned*. Three names and three ports
exist; no jails back them.

That is deliberate. The tier's purpose is to make "this environment cannot
promote" *structural* rather than procedural, and that job is done by the port
range and the IDP's refusal to lease =staging= — both of which exist now.
Provisioning jails for them would add three environments nothing currently
tests, to demonstrate a property already enforced.

*Trigger to build them:* the first time a team needs an environment that
outlives a worktree. Not before.

* Consequences

- The dashboard cannot be repointed at a per-service tool without first
  splitting the monorepo — which makes it a *standing reminder* of ADR 0001's
  options rather than a workaround for them.
- If we take option A or A2, *this ADR expires* and something MonoWatch-shaped
  becomes correct. Recorded so that is a recognised transition rather than a
  surprise.
- The tier boundary is enforced in exactly one place (=ports.sh=), so it is
  cheap to change and cheap to break. §Changing the rules is the relevant
  caution.
