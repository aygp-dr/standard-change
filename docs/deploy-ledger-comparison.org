#+TITLE: guile-deploy-ledger — prior art for the deployment record
#+SUBTITLE: What it already solves that this repo does not
#+DATE: 2026-09-13
#+OPTIONS: toc:2 num:t

* Why it matters here

=aygp-dr/guile-deploy-ledger= is a deployment tracking and change control
system in Guile. It is the closest existing thing to the *deployment record*
this model keeps needing and never has (=spec.org= §Domain model, capability
C6), and it independently arrived at three shapes this repo had to discover the
hard way.

* Its schema

#+begin_example
deployment-event: id service-name version environment deployment-type
                  started completed status initiator metadata parent-id
rollback-event:   id service-name from-version to-version
                  reason timestamp initiator impact deployment-id
#+end_example

* Three things it gets right that we had to find by failing

** started and completed are separate fields

Our TLA+ model had =DeployAndMerge= as *one atomic step*, which made the
=berths=2= race inexpressible; =sim/= found it by property testing and
=tla/Concurrent.tla= had to split the transition to state it at all.

The ledger has =started= and =completed= as distinct fields from the outset. A
deployment is an interval, not an instant — and every interesting failure in
this repo lives inside that interval.

** status is mutable — that is capability C5

=(status deployment-event-status set-deployment-event-status!)=

=docs/ci-portability.org= found that *verdicts revocable after issue* (C5) is
one of the two rare capabilities, native on only Azure and Jenkins among six
platforms. The ledger makes it a setter. A verdict that cannot be withdrawn
outlives its own truth, which is the whole of guard 4b.

** rollback is a separate event, not a status change

=rollback-event= carries =from-version=, =to-version=, =reason= and =impact=,
and references the =deployment-id= it undoes. The deployment event is *not*
rewritten.

That is the right call and we had not made it. A rollback that edits the deploy
record destroys the evidence that a bad version was ever live — which is
exactly what a post-implementation review needs. History is append-only or it
is not history.

* Two gaps it shares with us

** initiator is who claimed, not who observed

There is no field distinguishing an *observed* deployment from a *reported*
one. =initiator= records who started it, which is good provenance about the
claim and says nothing about the estate. This is the same C6 gap as GitHub's
Deployments API: *a platform can attest to what is running only if it runs it*,
and a ledger written by the deploying job is a self-report however well
structured.

Adding a =confidence= or =attested-by= field would close it, and is the shape
=docs/idp-api.org= §settlement already proposes.

** one version per event, so a mixed fleet is unrepresentable

=version= is singular. During a canary or a rolling deploy the estate serves
*two* versions, and =deployment-type= knowing the word "canary" does not let
the record say *what the split is*. That is the same limitation guard 5 had
before =experiments/004=, and the same one that makes Cloudflare's gradual
deployments hard to verify by sampling (§C6).

* What to adopt

| from the ledger                      | into this repo                              |
|--------------------------------------+---------------------------------------------|
| rollback as a separate append-only event | =change/rollback.sh= must RECORD, not rewrite |
| =from-version= / =to-version= / =reason= / =impact= | the PIR's rollback section     |
| =started= / =completed= as an interval | already forced on us by =sim/=; name it the same way |
| =deployment-type= as an enum          | blue-green is currently implicit in =switch.sh= |

And one thing to *not* adopt without extending: the singular =version=. Our
guard 5 now asserts convergence, so our record has to be able to say "two
builds, 90/10" or it will be less expressive than the check that feeds it.

* Status

Read-only comparison. Nothing in this repo depends on the ledger, and no guard,
