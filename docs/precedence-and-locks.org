#+TITLE: Precedence, locks and freezes — what other systems call these
#+DATE: 2026-09-13

* The distinction that started this

=itil:emergency= is *not* "I am ready to go out". It is *"if I am ready, I win
over anything else"*. A precedence claim, not a readiness claim.

That matters because the pipeline was using one label for three different
facts, and only one of them is about the change:

| fact                                        | about        | when |
|---------------------------------------------+--------------+------|
| this change outranks others                 | *the change* | from the moment it is classified |
| an emergency is deploying *right now*       | *the estate* | only while it runs |
| ordinary change is suspended                | *the estate* | until lifted |

=itil:emergency= is the first. It says nothing about the second or third, and
using it for them is why =sim/label_sim.py= found "'is there an emergency?'
cannot distinguish 'am I one' from 'is one running'".

* What other systems call each

** 1. Precedence — "I outrank you"

| system              | term                      |
|---------------------+---------------------------|
| ITIL 4              | *emergency change*, assessed by a separate authority (ECAB) |
| ServiceNow          | =Emergency= change type, plus a =priority= field, *kept separate* |
| Kubernetes          | =PriorityClass= + preemption |
| Linux / POSIX       | =nice=, real-time priority |
| Airflow, Celery     | task =priority_weight= / queue priority |

*Note what ServiceNow does*: change **type** and change **priority** are
different fields. An emergency change is a type; whether it preempts is a
priority. We collapsed those, which is the same collapse as class-vs-lifecycle.

** 2. In flight — "something is running, wait"

| system              | term                      |
|---------------------+---------------------------|
| Slack, Etsy, Chef   | *deploy lock* — "who has the lock?", "holding the lock" |
| Argo CD             | *sync window* |
| PagerDuty, Nagios   | *maintenance window* |
| Jenkins             | *Lockable Resources* plugin |
| databases           | *advisory lock*, *lease* |
| GitHub Actions      | =concurrency: group= — one run at a time |

*=lock= is the industry word*, and "holding the deploy lock" is idiom people
already have. It is also what guard 1 already is: the berth is a lock on the
path to production, wearing a =deploy:staging= label.

** 3. Suspended — "nothing goes, by policy"

| system              | term                      |
|---------------------+---------------------------|
| ITIL 4              | *change freeze*, *blackout window* |
| ServiceNow          | *blackout schedule* |
| retail / finance IT | *code freeze*, *peak freeze*, *quiet period* |
| security releases   | *embargo* |
| Argo CD             | a deny-type sync window |

=freeze= is the right word and we already use it.

* What follows for this repo

Three names, three subjects, and the test for each is *what question does it
answer*:

| label            | answers                              | subject | owner |
|------------------+--------------------------------------+---------+-------|
| =itil:emergency= | *what kind of change is this?*       | the PR  | a person |
| =lock=           | *is something deploying right now?*  | the estate | the pipeline, held for the duration |
| =freeze=         | *is ordinary change suspended?*      | the estate | a person |

** Why =lock= is not redundant with =deploy:staging=

=deploy:staging= is a lock *and* it is on the PR holding it, so "is the estate
busy?" is answered by scanning every open PR for a label. That works and it is
indirect — and it cannot express *"an emergency is deploying, so even the
things that normally proceed should not"*, because the label is scoped to one
berth.

=lock= would be estate-level and bare, symmetric with =freeze=: both are facts
about the world, neither is a property of any change.

** Why an emergency deploying is different from a berth being held

A held berth says *the path to production is busy*; wait your turn and nothing
about your change has changed. An emergency deploying says *production is being
changed outside the normal path* — your staging pass describes a world that is
being actively rewritten, so guard 4b will invalidate you whether or not you
wait politely.

They block for different reasons and the recovery is different: one is "wait",
the other is "wait and then re-verify".

* Rejected: =hold:staging=

A per-change hold — *"keep this in staging, I want to look"* — shipped as guard
4c and is *rejected as of 2026-09-13*. Recorded rather than deleted, because
the reasoning is the useful part.

*Why it looked necessary.* It was the only human stop the pipeline modelled.
Everything else was derived or automated, so a person who wanted to interrupt a
promotion had nowhere to say so.

*Why it is not.* The human stops were always there and they are *earlier*:

| to stop a change | use | scope |
|------------------+-----+-------|
| it should not go at all | *do not add =change:requested=* | that change |
| it is not fit to ship | *do not approve* | that change |
| nothing should go right now | *=freeze=* | the estate |
| this outranks everything | *=itil:emergency=* | the estate's ordering |

A change nobody wants promoted is simply *not requested and not approved*.
=hold:staging= added a fourth way to stop a deploy, with a fifth scope, and no
rule saying which applied when two were present.

*The specific hazard it left.* =freeze= and =hold:staging= both mean "do not
promote" and are owned by different writers at different scopes, so a change
could be frozen, held, both, or neither, and the four states had no ordering.
That is the two-writers-one-fact defect in a new place, and it is exactly what
=itil:*= and =change:*= were split to stop.

*What is lost.* The narrow case =hold:staging= served — *"I approve this, and
I want to look at staging before it promotes"* — is now expressed by not
approving until after looking. That is strictly slower and it is honest: the
approval then means what it says.

* Not decided here

Whether =lock= is worth adding, or whether scanning for =deploy:*= is enough,
is being determined by the simulator rather than argued — the same treatment
the bare-=emergency= question is getting. The requirement is a concrete state
where the ambiguity changes a verdict.

What *is* decided: =itil:emergency= is precedence, it is not readiness, and it
must not be used to answer a question about the estate.
