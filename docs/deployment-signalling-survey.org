#+TITLE: Deployment signalling labels, surveyed in situ
#+DATE: 2026-09-15

* What this asks

=docs/label-standards-survey.org= counted label *strings* GitHub-wide. This
asks a different question: *what does one team's complete signalling
vocabulary look like, and how do the pieces relate?* A count tells you
=deploy:staging= exists 169 times. It does not tell you what sits beside it.

Method: pull the full label set of repositories the earlier survey identified,
filter to deployment-adjacent names, read the descriptions. All =[E]=, measured
2026-09-15.

* Four shapes, not one vocabulary

The strings vary; the *shapes* are few, and a team picks one.

** 1. The per-environment triple — =nyrkio/nyrkio=

| label | description, verbatim |
|-------+-----------------------|
| =deploy:staging= | "Initiates deployment on staging" |
| =deployed:staging= | "State of successful staging deployment" |
| =deploy-failed:staging= | "State of failed staging deployment." |

*Intent, success, failure — three labels per environment*, and the intent/state
split is carried by the PREFIX (=deploy:= vs =deployed:=), independently
invented. Note the third: failure gets its own namespace (=deploy-failed:=)
rather than a suffix on the state one. Three prefixes, one environment suffix.

** 2. The release train — =openstax/rex-web=

=in-release-4=, =in-release-8=, =in-release-10= … =in-release-36=, plus
=in-staging= and =in-production=.

*No per-environment intent at all.* A change is "in release 28"; which
environment that is, is a property of the release, not of the change. The
environment labels exist but carry no intent — they are where the release got
to. This scales by adding a label per release forever, which is why there are
dozens.

** 3. The single-track queue — =mysociety/alaveteli=, =mysociety/foi-for-councils=

One label: =awaiting-deploy=. No environment, no success state, no failure
state. Either it is waiting or it is out.

*This is the honest minimum*, and it is worth noticing that a real project with
a real deployment cadence chose it. The information a second label would add is
recoverable from whether the issue is closed.

** 4. The gate-per-stage — =Coghatch-ai/lexflow=

The most developed vocabulary found, and the one worth studying. 40 labels,
*entirely prefixed*:

| prefix | members | what it answers |
|--------+---------+-----------------|
| =area:= | app, api, shared, drizzle, infra | WHAT MOVES |
| =sev:= | critical, high, medium, low | how bad |
| =review:= | pass, changes | did a human read it |
| =qa:= | pass, *blocked* | did the gate run |
| =uat:= | pass, fail, *blocked* | did acceptance run |
| =issuekit:= | in-progress, attempted, *blocked*, resolved | is anyone on it |

*** =area:*= is =app:*= under another name

Same job: derive what a change touches, from the paths. Two independent
projects reached "prefix a namespace, one member per deployable surface."

*** THREE NAMESPACES INDEPENDENTLY CARRY A THIRD VALUE

This is the finding.

- =uat:blocked= — "Acceptance could not run — precondition or environment"
- =qa:blocked= — "QA gate: blocked, needs fix before commit"
- =issuekit:blocked= — "Agent blocked — needs input / next hypothesis"

=uat:= is =pass / fail / blocked=. *Not pass/fail.* Somebody hit "could not
run" often enough to name it, in three separate namespaces, consistently.

That is the distinction =spec.org= defect class 8 argues for — /not yet is not
no, and cannot say is not either/ — arrived at by a team with no connection to
this work. It is the strongest external evidence the class is real rather than
an artifact of how this pipeline happens to be built, and it was reached the
same way: by being burned by a two-valued verdict.

Note also what =uat:blocked='s description names as the cause: *"precondition
or environment"*. The environment being wrong is a first-class reason a verdict
cannot be produced — which is exactly what =standard-change-004= is about.

* What this means for the per-environment tags

The premise was right: we cannot fully swap them out. But the survey says the
per-environment part is the *least* standardised thing here.

- Every project surveyed has a way to say *what a change contains* (=area:=,
  =app:=, =in-release-N=).
- Every project has a way to signal *intent to deploy* (=deploy:=,
  =needs-deploy=, =ready-to-deploy=, =awaiting-deploy=).
- *Only one of four* attaches the environment to the intent. Two omit
  environments from the signalling entirely.

So the two things a team reliably has are the two the earlier survey called
INTENT and MANIFEST. The environment suffix is the optional part, and the
projects that skip it are not less mature — =alaveteli= is fifteen years old.

* Also found, worth recording

- =Coghatch-ai/lexflow= =fixed-pending-deploy=: "Fixed; auto-closes on deploy".
  A label with a declared *lifecycle hook* — it names the event that removes
  it. Almost nothing else surveyed says who clears it.
- =safe-fndn/safe-singleton-factory= =ready-to-deploy=: "Pre-deployment checks
  have passed and is ready to…" — intent DERIVED from gate results rather than
  asserted by a person. The only instance of that direction found.

* Refutation

Four repositories is a sample, not a census, and they were chosen from a prior
survey that selected for having deployment labels at all — so "every project
has an intent signal" is true of *this sample by construction*. The claim that
survives is narrower: *where a deployment vocabulary exists, intent and
manifest are always present and the environment suffix often is not.*
