#+TITLE: Staging entry under gate flakiness -- what our checks cost, and what a red run does to the berth
#+DATE: 2026-09-15

Our gate checks, our label distribution, our singleton berth. Everything here
is measured from this repo or derived in closed form from it; the tooling and
the hypotheses it was built to test are =experiments/028-staging-entry-stress/=.

* The short version

1. *A 10% per-check failure rate is a 79.9% per-PR failure rate.* Entry to
   staging is conjunctive over ~16 checks. Nothing about a 10% check is
   survivable at that depth.
2. *Our check count is bimodal, so the mean lies.* Changes touch one app or
   all of them. Capacity planned on the mean n is wrong by 14%.
3. *A red run should release the berth.* Holding it while the author debugs
   costs throughput by exactly =1 + f·debug/hold=. At our numbers that is
   1.2× to 2.0×, and the advantage depends on nothing else at all.
4. *The workflow entry path cannot deploy anything today.* Two independent
   wiring defects, found while grounding the model. See §5.

* 1. What a change actually has to clear

From =.github/workflows/deploy-staging.yml= (six steps between claim and
verdict), =.github/workflows/gate.yml= (the four check-run names guard 4
requires green), and =router/routes.json= (what =gates/e2e.sh= probes):

: n  =  6 fixed steps  +  4 gate runs  +  1 per group installed  +  routes probed

Route counts per app, from =router/routes.json=: core 8, checkout 3, plp 2,
mock 2, pdp 1.

* 2. The label distribution, measured

87 PRs, every state, read 2026-09-15. This is the distribution *in practice*,
not an assumption:

| group set                    | PRs | n  | P(clean entry) at f=10% |
|------------------------------+-----+----+-------------------------|
| =(no app label)=             |  22 | --  | not a unit; never enters |
| =pdp=                        |  16 | 12 | 28.24%                  |
| =core=                       |  14 | 19 | 13.51%                  |
| =checkout=                   |  13 | 14 | 22.88%                  |
| =plp=                        |  10 | 13 | 25.42%                  |
| =checkout+core+pdp+plp=      |   7 | 28 | *5.23%*                 |
| =mock=                       |   2 | 13 | 25.42%                  |
| =checkout+core+mock+pdp+plp= |   2 | 31 | *3.82%*                 |
| =pdp+plp=                    |   1 | 15 | 20.59%                  |

65 of 87 are units (=CLAUDE.md=: a unit carries at least one =app:*= label).
The 22 without one never reach the berth and are excluded -- including them
would describe a population a quarter of which cannot enter staging at all.

*The shape is the finding.* n runs 12 to 31 with a hole in the middle: in 87
PRs, exactly one ever touched two apps. Changes here are single-app or
whole-estate, nothing between.

* 3. Why the mean is the wrong summary

| quantity | value |
|----------+-------|
| =E[(1-f)^n]= over the real mixture | *20.13%* pass |
| =(1-f)^E[n]= using mean n = 16.45  | 17.68% pass |
| gap | 2.45 points, 1.14× |

=x -> (1-f)^x= is convex, so the mixture always beats its own mean
(Jensen). With a bimodal n the two *diverge* instead of nearly agreeing. Plan
on the mean and you will under-provision by 14% -- in the safe direction for
capacity, but it also hides where the loss is: the single-app changes carry
the pass rate, and the seven-plus-two whole-estate changes are already lost
before anyone looks at them.

* 4. The berth, and the policy nobody has stated

=sim/simulate-gates.py= records an assumption that has never been argued for:

#+begin_quote
a red run holds the singleton berth while the author retries, so gate
flakiness converts directly into queue starvation
#+end_quote

That is one of two policies, and it is the worse one.

| policy | what a red run does |
|--------+---------------------|
| *HOLD* | author keeps the berth while debugging |
| *RELEASE* | berth frees immediately; author debugs off-berth and re-queues |

Attempts to green are Geometric(1−f), so per successful deployment:

: RELEASE   berth-minutes per green  =  hold / (1-f)
: HOLD      berth-minutes per green  =  hold + (f/(1-f)) * (hold + debug)

Dividing, everything about the queue cancels:

#+begin_quote
*RELEASE / HOLD  =  1 + f · debug / hold*
#+end_quote

No arrival rate, no backlog, no queue discipline, no fairness rule. The gain
depends only on how often runs go red and on how long debugging takes relative
to a deploy. It is ≥ 1 for every f ≥ 0: *releasing can never cost throughput.*

** Simulated against that, 1000 PRs, hold 30m, debug 60m

Saturated on purpose -- every change carries =release:start= and takes the
berth the moment it is free, the "if it's free I'm deploying" regime with no
calendar. That is the only regime where the policy difference is visible
rather than absorbed by idle time.

| f | policy | greens/day | analytic | err | tries | mean latency | p95 |
|---+--------+------------+----------+-----+-------+--------------+-----|
| 10% | release | 42.8 | 43.2 | 0.9% | 1.12 | 282h | 530h |
| 10% | hold    | 35.7 | 36.0 | 0.9% | 1.11 | 347h | 645h |
| 20% | release | 38.9 | 38.4 | 1.3% | 1.23 | 310h | 587h |
| 20% | hold    | 28.2 | 27.4 | 2.6% | 1.24 | 428h | 796h |
| 30% | release | 33.4 | 33.6 | 0.5% | 1.44 | 353h | 685h |
| 30% | hold    | 20.7 | 21.0 | 1.4% | 1.44 | 566h | 1087h |
| 40% | release | 28.7 | 28.8 | 0.5% | 1.67 | 414h | 795h |
| 40% | hold    | 16.1 | 16.0 | 0.6% | 1.66 | 738h | 1399h |
| 50% | release | 23.8 | 24.0 | 0.7% | 2.01 | 500h | 963h |
| 50% | hold    | 11.3 | 12.0 | *5.5%* | 2.08 | 1082h | 2019h |

Simulation matches the closed form everywhere. The one cell at 5.5% is
f=50% HOLD, which produces the fewest greens in the run and therefore the
widest Monte Carlo error; it is sampling noise, not disagreement -- it tightens
with =--prs 10000=.

** The ratio, three ways

| f | simulated | closed form | =1 + f·debug/hold= |
|---+-----------+-------------+--------------------|
| 10% | 1.200× | 1.200× | 1.200× |
| 20% | 1.382× | 1.400× | 1.400× |
| 30% | 1.614× | 1.600× | 1.600× |
| 40% | 1.781× | 1.800× | 1.800× |
| 50% | 2.102× | 2.000× | 2.000× |

*Latency is where it really shows.* At f=50% the mean time-to-green is 500
hours under RELEASE and 1082 hours under HOLD. Throughput doubles; waiting
doubles with it, and p95 goes from 963h to 2019h.

* 5. Two defects on this path, found while grounding the model

Neither was the object of the exercise. Both are on staging entry, so they
belong in this analysis rather than in a research note.

** D1 -- the workflow passes deploy groups where both targets read a sha

=spec.org:1574= and =docs/deployment-targets.org:102= both document
=deploy.sh <env> <groups...>=. *Both implementations disagree with the spec*,
and with each other about the consequence:

| target | signature | handed =groups.sh= output |
|--------+-----------+---------------------------|
| =targets/node/deploy.sh:37= | =sha="${2:?}"= | *refuses*, exit 3 |
| =targets/bastille/deploy.sh:10= | =sha="${2:-$(git rev-parse --short HEAD)}"= | *accepts*, writes it into =version.json= as the build sha |

=deploy-staging.yml:26= (and =deploy-production.yml:42=) pass
="$(./change/groups.sh "$PR")"= as =$2=. Measured against the real
=git rev-parse=, every possible =groups.sh= output is refused by the node
target -- including the empty string a non-unit produces:

: 'core'                    REFUSED     'checkout core pdp plp'   REFUSED
: ''                        REFUSED     '43a8e3e'                 OK

Two things follow. The node target *cannot ever* deploy through this workflow,
at any failure rate -- a deterministic 100% loss that no retry policy and no
gate reliability touches. And the Bastille target's silent acceptance puts a
group list where guard 5 expects a build identity.

The refusal arrives as exit 3, which =docs/exit-codes.org:15= defines as
"freeze, or the sha does not exist". *A wiring bug wearing a freeze's
clothes* -- the same confusion of categories as =guard4.sh:141=, where an empty
filter reads as consent.

=change/activate.sh:294= passes =targets/node/deploy.sh staging "$SHA"=
correctly. That is the path that built the live estate, and it is why this has
stayed invisible.

** D2 -- =vars.DEPLOY_TARGET= is not set

=gh variable list= returns =PRODUCTION_URL= and =STAGING_URL= only. The
workflow expands to =./targets//deploy.sh=. D2 stops the run before D1 can,
which is why D1 has never produced a visible failure.

*Neither is changed here.* Both sit on the same path as the deferred
=guard4.sh:141= item and belong with it in one ruling.

* 6. What follows for us

- *The 10–50% band is a per-PR band, and our per-check rate has to be far
  better than it sounds to reach it.* Inverting the mixture:

  | per-PR failure | implied per-check f | per-check pass |
  |----------------+---------------------+----------------|
  | 10% | 0.64% | 99.36% |
  | 20% | 1.36% | 98.64% |
  | 30% | 2.19% | 97.81% |
  | 40% | 3.14% | 96.86% |
  | 50% | 4.28% | 95.72% |

  A gate that is "99% reliable" puts us at a *15% per-PR failure rate*. This
  is the argument for =CLAUDE.md='s "zero findings or the gate failed" -- a
  warning tier is a check that fails 10% of the time on purpose.

- *Whole-estate changes are the expensive ones and we make them often.* Nine
  of 65 units touched four or five apps, at n=28–31 and a 4–5% clean-entry
  rate. Under the spec's own =deploy.sh <env> <groups...>= these would pay for
  what they touch. Under what is implemented, =deploy.sh= loops all five apps
  and =gates/e2e.sh= probes all sixteen routes regardless of the groups it is
  handed -- so a one-line =pdp= change pays the whole-estate toll anyway. D1 is
  the same divergence seen from the other end: *the groups argument is
  computed, passed, and then discarded by every consumer.*

- *State the berth policy.* RELEASE is free -- it never loses throughput at
  any failure rate, and it is worth 1.2×–2.0× at ours. If we want it, the
  place it becomes true is =deploy-staging.yml='s =always()= steps, which
  already release the lock and close the window; what is missing is that a red
  run currently leaves the *author* believing the berth is theirs to retry in.
  That is a documentation and =queue.sh= question, not a new mechanism.

* Reproducing

: ./experiments/028-staging-entry-stress/staging_entry_sim.py
: ./experiments/028-staging-entry-stress/staging_entry_sim.py --check
: ./experiments/028-staging-entry-stress/staging_entry_sim.py --sweep 10 25 50 --debug 120

=--check= asserts five independent agreements (Jensen's direction, the
constant-n collapse, the ratio identity, sim-vs-closed-form on six cells, and
that attempts-to-green is Geometric) and exits non-zero on any failure. A
Monte Carlo that agrees with nothing external is asserting its own output.

* What this does not model

Correlated failure (checks are independent here; real red runs cluster, which
makes the per-PR rate *better* and the queue *worse*), the calendar
(=preflight.sh= exit 3 and 4 are absent; both can only lower throughput),
debug time as anything but a constant, and any priority rule on the berth --
so =itil:emergency= preemption is not represented.
