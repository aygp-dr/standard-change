#+TITLE: Augmenting a repository with change-control tracking labels, without thrashing the labels it has
#+DATE: 2026-09-15

* What a repository must already have

Three things, under whatever names it uses. Nothing here renames them.

| it has                                 | here it is called   | examples elsewhere                                              |
|----------------------------------------+---------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| a risk designation on a change         | class, =itil:*=     | =risk:high=, =type:hotfix=, a Jira priority, a CAB category     |
| a concept of "something deployable"    | unit, =app:*=       | =component:*=, =area:*=, a CODEOWNERS path, a monorepo package  |
| pipelines that govern deployment       | the actions         | workflows, a CD service, a deploy script a person runs           |

The first two are the repository's own labels. They are *inputs* to change
control and never its state: derived from the diff or declared by a person,
written by the repository's own labeller, replaced by it on the next push.
The boundary is stated in full in the rebuild issue on namespace boundaries
(2026-09-15) and it is the one rule that keeps augmentation from becoming
replacement: *nothing in the tracking layer adds, removes or repairs a label
in those two namespaces.*

* The core case, then the scheduled case

Read this before the prefix table. Every row below it is a decision about
one of these two sequences.

** The core case: one word, then the process

A person opens a pull request and gets it reviewed. The labeller attaches
=app:*= and a class. The person adds one label: =release:start=. That is the
whole of their part. Review is finished before the word is said: the process
reads the approval as a precondition and never performs it, so no reviewer
appears in the flow below.

What happens next, in order. Each step is written by the thing that knows
the fact. The person watches it on the PR.

| step                                 | who acts                        | what the person sees on the PR                                                       | where it is documented                           |
|--------------------------------------+---------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------|
| the scheduler hears the word         | scheduler                       | a comment: heard; the berth is free, or held by #N                                   | five-properties P4; 023 §messaging audit         |
| the driver takes the berth           | driver                          | =release:start= goes, =deploy:staging= arrives                                       | five-properties P1; label-state-machine rule 5   |
| staging is installed and observed    | driver, then the instruments    | =staging:deployed=, then =staging:healthy=, then =staging:e2e= =staging:smoke= =staging:uat= | 004; 014; label-ownership rule 1; rule 14 |
| promotion                            | the forge                       | =deploy:production= lands on =staging:smoke= unless =release:hold= is present        | label-state-machine rule 13                      |
| production is installed and observed | driver, then the instrument     | =production:deployed=, then =production:healthy=                                     | 004                                              |
| settlement                           | the host that can see production | =release:completed=, the merge, the PIR comment, every marker cleared                 | label-ownership rule 2; rule 16                  |

The merge is the record. A shipped change ends with its =app:*= and class
labels, a PIR comment, and =mergedAt=. Nothing else stays on.

The process asks the person for one thing after start: a conflict the forge
cannot merge. A stale head is brought up to =main= by the forge (023 F1).
A held berth is waited on, and the wait is said once on the PR (023). The
person never runs a gate, deploys, approves or merges (023 §Verdict).

** Two more words the person may say

=release:hold= says "not further yet". The forge reads it at the moment it
would promote and lands nothing while it is present. Only the person lifts
it (label-state-machine rule 13; precedence-and-locks §Rejected).

=release:skip= says "the estate is untouched". The scheduler checks it
against the labeller: any =app:*= beside it is refused. With none, the gates
run on the head and the merge is the record. No berth, no window, no observation
(affected-by-construction; label-state-machine rule 17).

** Ending a release early

=release:end= is the person's word to stop a release that is in flight.
The pipeline closes the change with a code that names a fact:
=release:failed= when production never served it, =release:backed-out= when
it did and a person returned production to a named build. Every marker
goes. The window closes with the same code. The berth is released. The
class and =app:*= stay. The person's =release:start= is spent; saying it
again is a new release. Today the script is =change/abort.sh= and the word
is not wired; 024 F7 and F8 record what is still wrong on this path.

A release that fails on staging ends the same way, by the driver, with
nobody saying anything (§Every decision, "a change that failed at staging").

** The scheduled case: a calendar in front of the berth

A repository with a change calendar puts one step before the driver. The
person says =release:scheduled= instead of =release:start=. The scheduler
reads the estate first: is the berth free, is there an open window on this
environment, is the estate frozen. It books the first window that can hold
the work and writes =release:scheduled= with the window id. When the window
opens the guards run again, because reserving is not deploying
(five-properties P2; 003). Then the core case runs unchanged. A window that
lapses unattempted is closed =expired= by the reaper, =release:scheduled= is
removed, and the change waits for a person to ask again
(label-state-machine §Disagreements 3).

Two facts an adopter should carry. Most guards never read the calendar; 003
counts six of seven. What protects production is evidence bound to a build
and a singleton berth. And a window is an entitlement rather than an event:
about half the windows booked on the release night expired because nothing
drove them (003; time-spent).

* The mapping, not the rename

One table, kept in the repository, says how the tracking layer reads the
labels it does not own. Conceptually:

| tracking concept | read from                      | how                                                          |
|------------------+--------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------|
| standard         | their risk designation         | the values they call routine, pre-approved, low               |
| normal           | their risk designation         | the values that need a window or an approval                  |
| emergency        | a person's declaration         | a label a person adds; never derived                          |
| unit             | their deployable's label       | one or more; drives which gates and which targets             |
| environments     | their tiers                    | named as *places* (a port, a host, a slot), not runtime modes |

If the repository has no risk designation, the labeller's rule is the one
here: derive standard or normal from what the diff touches, and let a person
declare an emergency. If it has one, map it and leave it alone.

* The tracking layer that is added

Prefixed by writer. The prefix tells a reader who wrote a label, and a
person writes exactly one prefix; a rule that touches a prefix touches one
writer.

| prefix                                   | writer                                | members                                                                          |
|------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =release:*=                              | a person only                         | =start=, =hold=, =unaffected=, =scheduled=, =end=; any later word goes here      |
| =change:*=                               | the pipeline only                     | =scheduled=, =complete=, =end= (the act of clearing), the closure codes            |
| =deploy:*=, =<env>:deployed=             | the driver and the workflows          | the berth and the installs                                                        |
| =<env>:<instrument>=                     | the instrument that took it           | =healthy=, =e2e=, =smoke=, =uat=; on the next push they go                        |
| =blocked:*=                              | the guard that refused                | cleared by the same guard when it passes                                          |
| =freeze=, =emergency= on the estate issue | a person, about the estate            | facts about the world, on something that cannot be deployed; never on a PR        |
| =app:*=, =itil:*=                        | the labeller; a person for =emergency= | the repository's own, read and never written (§What a repository must already have) |

*standard-change provides the =release:*= primitives: a person declares
their intent about the release, and nothing else.* Three words are the base
and the only ones a change-control process built on this model requires:
=release:start=, =release:end=, =release:skip=. Nothing about a branching
model, an environment, a build identifier or a calendar changes what those
three mean, or is needed for them to work. Two more words are secondary,
adopted by a repository because of what it is building rather than because
the base process requires them: =release:hold= (a person wants to look
before promotion) and =release:schedule= (the estate has a calendar to book
against). The words name the intent and never the environment:
=release:hold= says "not further yet" wherever "further" is in the adopting
repository, where =staging:hold= would bind the person's word to one
repository's environment names, which change with the kind of repository
being supported (=history/promotion-models.org=: seven kinds behind one
word). All five are said about a branch, which is what makes branch
deployment legible: the branch is deployed and verified on the person's
word, and the merge follows the verification (GitHub flow's original order,
=history/pipeline-history.org= §Branching policies); the default branch
never needs a word, because reaching it is the record. Everything else is
written by the thing that knows the fact, and an observation is written
only by the instrument that measured it (=findings/label-ownership.org=,
experiments 014 and 023 F4). In this repository's tree on 2026-09-17 the
hold is still spelled =staging:hold=; the rename to the table above is
filed for the two operators to make together.

* Augmenting in five steps

1. *Do not rename.* Map the risk designation and the deployable label in the
   table above. The audit that ships with this layer refuses any write to
   those namespaces from any file but the repository's labeller.
2. *Declare owners before writing a label.* One row per label: who writes it,
   who clears it, whether a person may. The pattern is
   =change/label-owners.tsv=; a label nobody owns is a label nobody can
   trust (=label-ownership=).
3. *Give the person one word.* =release:start=. Everything the pipeline needs
   after that it derives or observes. A second human word is a second thing
   to explain (023: eleven confusions, most of them words).
4. *Let the instrument write the observation,* keyed to the build it saw. A
   label with no build attached authorizes nothing (014).
5. *End every release,* including the ones that fail: clear the working
   labels, keep the closure code, leave the merge as the record.

* What the models check

The TLA+ module and the simulator (=label-state-machine=) check the tracking
layer only: seventeen switchable rules, each with an invariant it protects,
over two changes and one estate. The repository's own labels enter as
constants (the class, the units). A rebuild that keeps the boundary can re-run
both against its own scripts without modelling the labeller at all.
