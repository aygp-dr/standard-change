#+TITLE: Labels are the only channel, and nobody is coordinating
#+DATE: 2026-09-15

* The situation being modelled

Any number of actors act on one pull request. None of them can see the others.
They do not hold a session, share a lock file, or exchange messages. **The
labels are the entire communication channel**, and every actor may add or
remove any label at any moment.

Observed on this estate: a human, a CI/CD workflow (=github-actions[bot]=), a
scheduled process (=change/scheduler.sh=), a driver on hydra and a driver on
the mini all wrote =deploy:staging= on the same day. None of them was wrong.

* What this means for the models

** OneLifecycle is not a safety invariant

=tla/Labels.tla= checks =OneLifecycle== -- at most one active lifecycle label.
Under arbitrary mutation *that cannot hold*, and no guard on any single writer
can make it hold, because the violation is produced by a writer the guard does
not control. Trying to enforce it by constraining =Request=, =Book= and
=Start= one at a time is chasing a property that belongs to a system nobody is
running.

It is a *convergence* property: the pipeline should return to one active
lifecycle label, and should not do anything dangerous while it has two.

** The invariants that DO survive arbitrary labelling

These are about *actions*, not about label sets, and they are the ones worth
proving:

| invariant | why it survives |
|-----------+-----------------|
| =NoDraftDeployed= | a deploy is an action; no label combination authorizes it on a draft |
| =NoDeployWithTwoClasses= | the refusal happens at the deploy, not at the labelling |
| =NoUnbookedDeploy= | same shape |
| =VerdictOnHealthy= | a verdict is written from a measurement, not from a label |
| =MergedHasRecord= | the record is the forge's, not a label |

*The distinction is the whole design.* A label may say anything at any time.
What must be true is that **no dangerous action follows from a label alone** -- 
which is exactly why guard 4 reads observation RECORDS that name a build, and
why a label cannot name a build.

** What the simulator must therefore do

Admit an =Interfere= step: any actor adds or removes any label at any time,
between any two steps of any driver. The simulator must not crash, must not
deadlock, and must not take a dangerous action. A simulator that only exercises
well-behaved writers is testing a system nobody deployed.

* The corollary for the pipeline

*Nothing that reads a label may treat it as authorization.* It is a hint about
where a change probably is. The authority is:

- an observation record, which names the build it measured;
- the estate itself, asked at the moment of reliance;
- the forge's own merge state.

Every defect in =scenarios.org= that involves a stale, duplicated or swept
label is a place where something treated the channel as the record.
