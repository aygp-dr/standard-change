#+TITLE: Modelling release:*: the options, and what each lets the model say
#+DATE: 2026-09-15

The owner's question of 2026-09-15: a developer, release engineer or
operator owns the =release:*= tags, even when a tool applies them on their
behalf. Should the words follow the Ruby state-machine convention, a verb for
the event and a past participle for the state? Four options were on the
table. The decision for rebuild 005 is the first; the tree keeps its current
spellings until then, with one rename made now (=release:unaffected= is
=release:skip=, a verb like the others).

* Option 1: one namespace, two tenses (chosen for rebuild 005)

The person says the verb; the runner answers the participle. The tense is the
writer, so a reader needs no owner table to know who wrote a label.

| the person says (a verb) | the runner answers (a participle) | the pair means                                                   |
|--------------------------+-----------------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =release:start=          | =release:started=                 | the verb was consumed; a driver holds this change                |
| =release:schedule=       | =release:scheduled=               | a window is booked; the id is in the comment                     |
| =release:hold=           | =release:held=                    | promotion was refused while the hold is on; the person lifts it  |
| =release:end=            | =release:ended=                   | every marker cleared; a closure code says why                    |
| =release:skip=           | =release:skipped=                 | the estate untouched, the labeller agreed, the merge is the record |
|                          | =release:completed=               | production converged on this head; merge and PIR follow          |
|                          | =release:failed=, =release:backed-out=, =release:abandoned=, =release:superseded= | closure codes; they stay |

Three properties fall out, and each is an invariant the model can check.

- *The mismatch is the signal.* A verb with no participle beside it means no
  runner is listening. That is the fact a person needs first, and the one
  the 024 owners could not get: a dead scheduler leaves its last
  reassurance behind and nothing on the ticket says it is dead. Under this
  grammar the ticket says it by omission, which a poll can read.
- *A participle exists only because a runner wrote it.* One tag never does
  both jobs. The owner's phrasing: "not that a single tag does the same
  thing unless we had a runner." A label that is both the request and the
  record is the defect 014 found in another form, a claim with no
  measurement behind it.
- *The verb is consumed.* A verb that stays on re-fires (023 F6 started two
  drivers from one start). The runner removes the verb as it writes the
  participle, in one edit where the forge allows it.

Nothing in =release:*= is about a build or an environment. =deploy:*=,
=<env>:deployed= and the observations stay as the instruments' facts. The
estate's =freeze= and =emergency= stay on the estate issue.

* Option 2: two prefixes, the prefix is the writer (issue #125)

=release:*= for the person's words, =change:*= for the pipeline's record.
Simpler to audit by file (nothing but the driver consumes =release:*=), and
it is what the tree partly has today. Its cost is the second namespace: a
reader must know which prefix is which, and the pipeline's answer to a verb
lives under a different prefix from the verb, so the mismatch signal is a
join across two names rather than a glance at one.

* Option 3: one tag per step, no answer

=release:start= consumed, =deploy:staging= as the visible effect, no
acknowledgement. This is the tree as it ran in 023. Five of five owners
reported the silence after saying start as their first confusion, and the
scheduler was given a comment to say "heard". A comment is not a label; a
poll cannot read it and a second operator's tooling cannot either. Rejected
by the runs.

* Option 4: labels plus a record elsewhere

The lock record on the estate issue, the evidence comments, the PIR. These
are not alternatives to the tags; they are what carries the values a tag
cannot (a build sha, a window id, a reason). The rule that keeps them from
competing with the tags: a tag is a fact about the change's state, and a
record is what the fact was measured against. D21 is what happens when the
lock is only a tag.

* How option 1 is modelled

The model's state becomes the set of labels on each pull request, plus the
forge's own =merged=, plus the estate's two labels. Every hidden fact the
current model carries (=served=, =tidied=, the history flags) is either a
participle the runner writes or a check on a transition. That is the
owner's "model only from the PR tag perspective".

- Each pair is two booleans per change, =verb= and =participle=. The
  person's actions set verbs; only runner actions set participles, and every
  runner action that sets a participle clears the matching verb.
- Invariants that become stateable: =ParticipleHasRunner= (no participle is
  ever set by a person's action; with the Interfere rule on, this is the
  invariant that fails, which is the point), =VerbConsumed= (no verb survives
  the runner action that answers it), =OneAnswer= (at most one participle of
  a pair is on), and =NoAnswerWithoutAsk= (=started= implies a =start= was
  consumed on this head, which needs the head as part of the state, as 014
  needs it for observations).
- Closure codes are participles with no verb: the runner's outcomes. The
  invariant =CodeIsTerminal= says no verb is accepted on a change that
  carries one, which is what "the intent is spent" means.
- The estate stays outside the change's tuple: =freeze= and =emergency= are
  read by =Activate= and never written by a change's action.
- The seventeen rules survive the rename with their constants; what changes
  is that the label strings in comments match the labels a reader sees.

* What the current tree does with it

Nothing yet beyond the one rename. The model keeps =release:start= (the verb),
=life= (the pipeline's lifecycle set), =hold=, =unaffected= and =cleaned=
as separate variables, which is option 2 in structure. The skills
=/release-start=, =/release-schedule= and =/release-end= already speak
option 1 from the person's side, since a skill is named for the verb it
says. The rename lands with rebuild 005, and the six-thing mandate applies:
one rule, one invariant, one negative, the simulator's row, the owner row,
the doc row.
