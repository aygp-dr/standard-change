#+TITLE: 027 -- what the mainline review cost, per agent, and what the cheap driver actually did
#+DATE: 2026-09-16

* Hypothesis

Splitting a review into a specification by an expensive model and a
mechanical walk by a cheap one costs an order of magnitude less than the
expensive model doing both, and produces a ledger of the same quality.
Falsified if the cheap walk's cost is within a factor of three of the
specification's, or if the ledger it produces would need to be redone.

* Success criterion

A per-agent table from the agents' own transcripts (017's counting rule),
and a check of the walk against its own procedure: tool uses per row, and
whether the per-row steps the specification names were run.

* Run

#+begin_example
python3 experiments/027-review-cost/tally.py \
  "l7-spec (opus)=<task>/a21e71825a9cbcf0e.output" \
  "driver (haiku)=<task>/a2990bd6cfc6ef04a.output" \
  "split-assessor (sonnet)=<task>/a410e3c77126ce94d.output"
#+end_example

Rates are the table in =tally.py=, USD per million tokens, approximate;
edit them when they change.

The specification cost 22 times the walk. The first clause of the
hypothesis holds.

* F1 -- the cheap walk did not walk

The ledger has 185 rows and the driver made 35 tool uses in total, which is
one tool use per five rows. The specification's Step 7 (the survival check:
grep three added lines at HEAD) and Step 6 (re-run a cheap reproduction) are
per-row commands; at that count they were not run per row. The verdicts
show it: 171 of 185 rows are =unverifiable=, and rows such as =9332ed3= (a
one-line CSS rule still in the tree) carry "no corroborating evidence
found" when a single =git grep= finds the evidence. The seven
=confirmed-by-note-only= rows are the rows the extractor had already marked
as carrying a complete note. The walk re-expressed the mechanical columns
as verdicts and cost forty cents doing it.

The second clause of the hypothesis fails: the ledger's judgement columns
would need to be redone. The fix is the one the specification hinted at
when it made the survival check "mechanical and cheap": a check that is
mechanical belongs in the extractor, where it runs for every row without a
model in the loop. Steps 6 and 7 move into =extract.py=; the model's share
of the walk shrinks to the rows the mechanics cannot settle.

* F2 -- where the money is, again

As in 017: cache reads are 92 to 96 percent of every agent's tokens. The
L7's 35 tool uses read the spec, the methodology, sixty commits and four
notes into context once each; the walk read the ledger and its caches. The
lever is the number of turns and what each turn re-reads, and the cheap
model's saving came from making fewer turns, which is also why it skipped
the rows.

* What this changes

- =experiments/026-mainline-review/extract.py= gains the survival check and
  the cheap re-run, so the mechanical verdicts (=no-claim=,
  =confirmed-by-note-only=, and =confirmed= by survival) need no agent.
- A driver agent is given rows, one per turn, with the row's evidence in the
  prompt, or it is not given the walk at all. "Follow the procedure" over
  185 rows is a request a cheap model answers by summarising.
- Done the same day: =extract.py --mechanical= settles 178 of 185 rows in
  7.5 seconds (85 confirmed by survival, 84 unverifiable of which 50 are the
  protocol defect, 10 by a complete note, 7 no-claim), against the driver's
  171 unverifiable at USD 0.41. The cheapest model for a mechanical check is
  no model.
