#+TITLE: 024 — five owners, one task each: what the PR could not tell them
#+DATE: 2026-09-15

* Setup

Five agents, one pull request each (#52 core, #55 core, #60 plp, #61 pdp,
#70 checkout). Each was told: *get your change to production*. Each was a
DEVELOPER who owns one change, forbidden from editing any pipeline script,
workflow, gate or spec — if something is broken, record it. Forbidden from
clearing another change's label, because that is D21 and it is what we are
studying. 25-minute bound; a stall is a result.

They were told to use =change/driver.sh=. *That instruction was wrong, and it
is finding F1.*

* Result

| PR | landed? | how |
|----+---------+-----|
| #52 | *yes* | by ABANDONING the driver and hand-running the eight-step node sequence |
| #55 | no | two driver runs, both aborted at the same step |
| #60 | no | ready and approved; ten minutes of silence with the berth free |
| #61 | no | blocked by a false guard-2 refusal (F6) |
| #70 | no | blocked on a calendar slot 43 minutes out, on an idle estate |

*One of five landed, and only by leaving the documented path.*

* Findings

** F1 — the entry point belongs to a different machine, and nothing says so

=change/driver.sh= hardcodes =STG=http://127.0.0.1:9010=,
=PROD=:9020=, =worktrees/staging=, =worktrees/production= and
=/tmp/claude-501/= — uid 501, the mini. On hydra none of those paths exist.
There is no environment override; =STG= and =PROD= are constants.

All five agents hit it. Four had to read the script to find out.

*The near-miss is worse than the failure.* =:9010= and =:9020= ARE listening
on hydra — as =dev-1= and =dev-2=, =tier=dev=, =promotes=no=. At the time of
the run =:9010= was serving =b72589f=, *which was #60's own pre-rebase head*.
Had =worktrees/staging= existed, the driver would have deployed a protected
change onto a dev block, and the health poll would have MATCHED — reporting
staging deployed while having deployed nothing. A live false-pass, one SHA
away.

** F2 — the abort names a symptom and never the cause

Every failed run posted the same comment:

: Change closed: failed
: Reason: driver: deploy staging -- staging did not come up serving <sha>

This is false as a diagnosis. Staging was healthy on =:9200= throughout; #52
passed 5/5 health, 27 e2e checks and a 15-url smoke crawl on it minutes later.
The cause — =fatal: cannot change to 'worktrees/staging': No such file or
directory= — reached the terminal and *never the pull request*.

Acting on the comment alone, an owner investigates a working estate.

** F3 — the refusal that mattered most was never posted at all

=driver.sh= exit 5, "lock held by #N", goes to stdout only. No comment, no
label, no queue entry. =README.org= promises the opposite: /"leaves one
comment naming the holder"/ — that is =queue.sh=, and =driver.sh= bypasses it.

An owner who says =change:start= and walks away sees a change that silently
does nothing, forever, with no queue position and no bound. All five agents
hand-rolled a polling loop. Three lost races they could not see.

** F4 — a promise kept by nothing

=change/scheduler.sh:36= posts: /"This ticket is retried every 20s and takes
the berth when it is free; nothing is asked of you."/ On #61 the scheduler
that posted it died of SIGPIPE a minute later. The comment stands; nothing is
retrying; the PR cannot say so.

That is the worst stall in the run, because the system actively reassures you
while nothing happens. Silence and success look identical; so now do silence
and a dead daemon.

(#60 reported this text "exists nowhere in the checkout". It does —
=scheduler.sh:36=, with =${INTERVAL}s= interpolated, so a grep for the
rendered string misses it. The claim as stated is wrong; the finding is not.)

** F5 — guard 1 admitted two holders

: 02:51:42Z  #52  +deploy:staging  by=aygp-dr
: 02:51:42Z  #55  +deploy:staging  by=aygp-dr
: 02:51:53Z  #55  -deploy:staging  by=github-actions[bot]

Two changes held the berth in the same second. There are TWO locks — the
repo-wide label and =change/lock.sh='s record — acquired non-atomically, so
both drivers passed the check in the gap. =deploy-staging.yml= arbitrated
eleven seconds later by stripping one label, while its driver went on
believing it held the berth.

This upgrades D20. It is not that guard 1 has never refused; it is that guard
1 *admitted a violation of the property it exists to enforce.*

Three readings of one lock disagreed across the run: =lock.sh status= said
=pr: 52= while a PR comment said the berth was held by #55, five seconds
apart.

** F6 — a guard that refuses because it cannot see past page one

=change/activate.sh:200= fetches =repos/$R/commits/$SHA/check-runs= with no
=per_page= and no pagination. Measured on #61's head =6a954d3=:

| request | total_count | returned | gate-selftest found |
|---------+-------------+----------+---------------------|
| default | 35 | 30 | *0* |
| =?per_page=100= | 35 | 35 | 1 |

Once a SHA carries more than thirty check runs, =gate-selftest='s success
falls onto page two and guard 2 refuses: /"gate-selftest did not pass and no
local/ self-test either; gate results are void."/

The refusal is false, and it sits under a comment in the same file warning
that /"a guard that reads a stale attempt is wrong in the direction that looks
safe"/. Same shape, different cause.

*The escape hatch it offers cannot be taken.* =local/gate-selftest= is READ by
=activate.sh=, =guard4.sh= and =gates/preflight.sh=, and WRITTEN by nothing in
the repository. No command produces it.

** F7 — the abort consumes the human intent

=abort.sh= strips =change:start=. So every infrastructure failure — a missing
worktree on the wrong host — costs a HUMAN ACT to restate an intent that never
changed. #55 paid it twice, #60 once.

=change:start= exists to be the one thing a person must say. A machine fault
should not spend it.

** F8 — =change:end= does not distinguish landed from failed

#70 carries it after a clean landing. #55 carries it after two aborts. The
tombstone records that cleanup RAN, not what happened. That is my defect: I
declared the label on 2026-09-14 with =persistent=yes= and no outcome.

** F9 — smaller, each verified

- =schedule.sh= refuses with /"Book 8m or more"/ while =QUANTUM= defaults to
  30; the advice cannot be followed. Three bookings asked for 20m/20m/10m and
  got 30m *silently*.
- =--at= silently relocated a booking: 02:52 for 15m became 03:00–03:30.
- Next-available returned 2026-09-19 for a calendar empty from 03:30 today: it
  appends after the furthest-out booking, and dead windows from 22 hours
  earlier still read =open= because nothing reaps them.
- =settle.sh= printed =ok berth released= while =lock.sh status= still read
  =pr: 52= eight seconds later. It releases the LABEL and leaves the RECORD.
- =gates/uat.sh= exits 2 with no diagnosis — two =installing (once)= lines and
  nothing else. It is the FreeBSD/Playwright blocker, unstated. =driver.sh=
  calls it unconditionally, so *even with the worktrees present the driver
  could not have completed on hydra*.
- =driver.sh= would have self-approved: it hardcodes =gh auth token --user
  aygp-dr=, and #52's author IS =aygp-dr=, so guard 4 would have refused a
  void approval.
- =queue.sh= with no arguments prints a raw =sh= error and exits 0.
- A mis-booking has no cancel verb exposed to the booker; #61 was left holding
  two open windows.
- Nothing merged between 00:11:57Z and 02:56Z — 2h44m — while drivers churned
  every ~90s. The estate looked busy and landed nothing.

* What the pipeline already does right, and it is the template

The single best message in the run was written by a PERSON: the
=CHANGES_REQUESTED= review on #60 from 2026-09-14. It named the rule, quoted
both versions of the line, explained WHY (#38 renamed =d.results= to
=d.items=), stated /"What would clear this: rebase onto main and re-express
the ternary against d.items.length"/, and separated what it had checked from
what it had not. #60's owner acted on it *without opening a single script*.

Two machine refusals reach that standard:

- =production-first.sh=: /"This check re-runs on every label change, so it
  turns green by itself the moment gates/health.sh records production:healthy.
  Nothing to re-push."/ — says what to do AND what not to bother doing.
- =activate.sh= guard 3: =FAIL= + reason + /"The berth was NOT claimed;
  nothing is blocked behind this."/ + a =Recovery:= line.
- =review.sh --approve= printed its reasoning, named both identities, and
  re-read the forge to confirm.

The pipeline knows how to write a refusal. The abort path and the lock path
were not given the same care.

* The one change, named independently by all five

*Every refusal must post a comment, name the command that failed and its
stderr rather than an inferred symptom, and end with the next action in the
imperative.*

Two of the five put the lock refusal first and the abort second; three the
reverse. Nobody nominated anything else.
