* Hypothesis

/The four DORA metrics can be derived from what this repo already records,
without estimating any of them./

*Falsifiable as stated.* It is refuted if any of the four requires a number
nobody measured. It is *partly* refuted — change failure rate and time to
restore are derivable and defensible; *authorization* and *blast radius* are
not, and the reason is structural rather than an oversight. See §What the data
cannot tell us.

*Success criterion.* Every figure below traces to a row in =data/=, and the
derivation re-runs to the same numbers from the committed snapshots.

* 1. Deployment frequency

*17 production cutovers that served. 18 production deployment attempts.*

- window: =2026-09-13T15:42:05Z= .. =2026-09-14T02:11:09Z= = *10h29m04s*
- rate: *1.62 per hour*, one every *37.0 minutes*
- median gap between consecutive cutovers: *12m32s*
- by UTC calendar day: *11* on 2026-09-13, *6* on 2026-09-14

Counted as *cutovers, not merges*, per the brief. The unit is "the front was
pointed at a new build", which is the moment a build begins serving. That is
also why the count is 17 and not 19: two *staging* windows closed =passed=
(=CHG-20260913T2030-40.1=, =CHG-20260913T2030-42.1=) without any production
cutover following them. A green staging window is not a deployment.

*The clock.* The PIR timestamp is the fallback, because it is the only record
present for all 17. It is posted at *settlement*, a few seconds after the front
actually flips — measured, not assumed: across the *7* cutovers the mirror
witnessed, the PIR trails the flip by *5.4s to 10.5s*, median *9.4s*. Where the
mirror covers the event its timestamp is used instead, so 7 of the 17 rows below
are instrument-timed and 10 are settlement-timed with that ~9s bias baked in.
One outlier is worth naming: #9's PIR is at 15:42:05Z but its cutover
comment is at *15:32:14Z*, 9m51s earlier, because the =production:healthy=
withdrawal loop ran four times before settling. #9's lead time below is
therefore ~10 minutes long.

*An anomaly in the ledger, recorded not smoothed.* #39 (19:10Z) and #43
(19:22Z) both cut over to *blue*. Blue/green is supposed to alternate, and the
PIRs show it: #43's rollback target is =73423f0= on green, the same target #39
named. Two consecutive deploys to the live colour means #43 had no clean
rollback target of its own — the build it would have returned to was the one it
had just overwritten. Nothing failed as a result. It is in the data and the
schema below can express it; the current one cannot.

* 2. Lead time for changes

*Median 2h40m59s. Mean 3h06m27s. p90 6h10m38s. Range 5m51s .. 10h00m36s.*

Measured from the *earliest author date* among the PR's commits, to the
production-serving moment above. n = 17.

** Which commit, and why

The brief says several branches were rebased, "which resets author date but not
the work". *The data says the opposite, and it is worth correcting because it
changes which column you measure.*

Seven branches — #6, #28, #33, #38, #40, #42, #44 — carry *committer* dates
inside a twelve-second band at =23:35:55Z=..=23:36:07Z=, while their *author*
dates span =13:41:03Z=..=19:37:33Z=. That is an ordinary =git rebase=: author
date preserved, committer date rewritten. The rebase is what happened at
23:35Z; the author dates are what happened during the day.

So *author date measures the work and committer date measures the rebase*, and
using committer date would produce this:

| PR | from author date | from committer date |
|----+------------------+---------------------|
| #6 | 10h00m36s | 5m44s |
| #33 | 6h10m38s | 25m16s |
| #38 | 5h09m57s | 15m13s |
| #28 | 6h28m40s | 2h15m58s |

— a median of about 45 minutes instead of 2h41m, achieved by deleting the day.
Neither column is *wrong*; one of them answers the DORA question.

* 3. Change failure rate

*3 of 18 production deployment attempts = 16.7%.*

** The definition, and the defence

#+begin_quote
A *change failure* is a production deployment that required *unplanned
remediation* — the front was rolled back, or the deployment record was
withdrawn because the build never served. The denominator is production
deployment *attempts*.
#+end_quote

Four decisions in that sentence, each of which could have gone the other way.

*1. The denominator is production deployments, not merges and not windows.*
DORA's ratio is about deployments. 21 PRs merged tonight and only 17 reached
production; counting merges would measure something else and flatter the rate.

*2. An expired window is not a failed change.* =spec.org= §Boundary conditions
is unambiguous — /expired: a reservation lapsed unattempted. *not a failure.*
Its own closure code; the change keeps everything else/ — and =reap.sh= says it
in every comment it posts. *13 windows expired tonight* and not one of them
enters either side of the ratio: nothing was attempted, so nothing failed, and
no deployment happened, so there is no denominator entry either. Folding them
in would give 16/34 = 47%, which would describe the *slot length* and call it
the software's quality. Keeping them out is the whole reason =expired= is a
distinct closure code, and the schema below refuses to let them merge.

*3. Cancelled is not failed either, and mostly is not even a decision.* 277
windows closed =cancelled=, but *229 of them belong to three PRs* (#6: 81,
#42: 78, #28: 70) — the runaway booker of scenario D12, re-booking faster than
anything could drive. That is queue churn. Treating it as change outcomes would
make the night look catastrophic while measuring a loop.

*4. The four =failed= windows are staging, and a gate that refuses is the
control working.* All 326 windows are =env=staging=. A staging gate catching a
bad build *before* production is the pipeline succeeding, and #56 — the
deliberately broken change of D14 — is the clearest case: refused, window
lapsed, nothing deployed. Counting it as a change failure would penalise the
repo for its central claim holding.

|---+----+-------+------+---------------|
| 1 | #42 | =2567015= | 09-13T19:43:50Z | production deployment record opened, =success= posted at 19:43:59 "observed by gates/health.sh", then =failure= at *19:44:34Z*: /WITHDRAWN: 2567015 is not serving anywhere in the estate./ A deployment that reported success and placed nothing. |

| 3 | #54 | =df22f7a= | 09-14T02:11:09Z | the driver piped =guard4.sh= to =tail=, so =set -e= saw =tail='s exit status and walked past a =NOT authorized= into production (D16). Rolled back at *02:11:57Z*. |

** The two that deliberately do not count

*The emergency.* =emergency= was declared on the estate at *22:55:13Z* and
cleared at *23:07:11Z* — *11m58s*, during which =preflight.sh= refused every
standard and normal change, verified live in the record on issue #1. #50 was
reclassified =itil:emergency= and merged at 23:04Z. It is *not* in either side
of the ratio, because *#50 deployed nothing*: =change/groups.sh 50= returns
empty, it touches no file under =apps/=, and =production-first.sh= exempts it.
It is a control-plane change, and for one of those production *is* =main=.
An emergency is a classification, not an outcome; the estate flag that stopped
everyone else is a property of the *world*, not of #50. Conflating the two is
the exact defect =spec.org= §Nomenclature says took longest to find.

(There is an earlier =emergency= on/off pair at 21:07:24Z → 21:08:27Z, 63
seconds, with no change attached — a dashboard exercise, not an event.)

*Three variants of the number, so the choice is visible:*

| framing | value |
|---------+-------|
| unplanned remediation / attempts (*chosen*) | *3/18 = 16.7%* |
| rollbacks / cutovers that served | 2/17 = 11.8% |
| user-visible degradation / attempts | *0/18 = 0.0%* |

The last one is real and should be said plainly: *no user-facing defect reached
production tonight.* #54 was one CSS rule; #40's build was fine and its problem
was trunk divergence; #42's build never served at all. What failed all three
times was the *control*, not the software — and that is precisely what a change
failure rate over a gated pipeline ought to be measuring, which is why 16.7% is
the headline and not 0%.

Measured from =snapshot.live_sha= in the dashboard mirror, which polls the front

build and left *one command away* from the bad one. =43646f9= was only

incidentally, not as remediation.

*A correction to =scenarios.org= D16.* It records #54's exposure as "roughly
ninety seconds". The mirror says *47.1 seconds* — first sighting 02:11:09.999Z,
last sighting 02:11:56.064Z, restored 02:11:57.073Z, with ~1s polls throughout
and no gap. The narrative number is high by a factor of two. Recorded here
rather than edited into D16, because the observation belongs to whoever amends

*What cannot be measured:* #42's withdrawn deployment at 19:43Z has no

different quantity and is not reported as TTR.

* What the data cannot tell us

Three things, none of which is a gap in effort.

** 1. Whether any production deployment was authorized

*Every one of the 326 windows is =env=staging=.* There is no production
reservation anywhere in =refs/idp/schedule=. So the question DORA cares least
about and change management cares most about — /was this cutover inside an
approved window, and who approved it?/ — *cannot be answered by a query*, only
by reading prose.

Worse, and this is the finding: *guard verdicts are printed, never persisted*.
Guard 4 refused #54 at 02:10Z and the refusal exists nowhere but a terminal and
a scenario write-up. In the stored record, #54's cutover is byte-for-byte
indistinguishable from an authorized one. An audit run against this data would
report 17/17 clean. The =change_authorization= table and the
=bc_refusal_ignored= view below exist entirely to make that state writable.

** 2. Whether anything went wrong before 23:45:58Z

The dashboard mirror — the only independent witness to what production actually
served — starts at =2026-09-13T23:45:58Z=. *Eleven of the seventeen cutovers
happened before it was running*, and rest on PIR self-report alone.

That is not a small caveat, because in the ~2h54m the mirror *was* running, PIR
self-report was wrong *twice* (D13 claimed a merge that did not happen; D16
reported a refused cutover as success). A rollback before 23:45Z would leave no
trace in any source I have. *The 16.7% is a lower bound*, and if the earlier
hours resembled the later ones it is a substantial underestimate.

** 3. Blast radius, for the changes that most needed it

=app:*= labels are the blast-radius signal, and *#40 and #44 reached production
with none* — their evidence-at-completion lines read =itil:standard
change:scheduled staging:* review:proxy= and nothing more. Both are =noop/*=
branches that later grew real content. So for 2 of 17 cutovers the radius is
*unrecordable*, not zero — and =CLAUDE.md= defines a unit as "one pull request
carrying at least one =app:*= label", which neither was.

Separately, classification is recorded in *two vocabularies*: #9, #11, #19 and
#31 carry =change:standard= / =change:normal=, everything after 17:40Z carries
=itil:standard= / =itil:normal=. I normalised them in =derive.py=, which is a
report-time judgement that a store should never have required.

* The schema

=schema.sql=, PostgreSQL 14+. *[H] — the DDL has not been executed; no
PostgreSQL is installed on hydra.* Table ordering and forward references were
checked mechanically; the constraint bodies are unverified and stay =[H]= until
a cell runs.

It is built on =docs/interfaces.org= §7 and keeps most of it.

** Where §7 is right and this keeps it verbatim

- *=classification= and =lifecycle= as separate columns.* Different subjects,
  different questions. Tonight proves it: #50 was =itil:emergency= (a class) and
  =complete= (a lifecycle) and the estate was in =emergency= (a third subject
  entirely, on =estate_flag=, attached to nothing deployable).
- *=closure_code= closed, with =expired= ≠ =failed=.* The single most
  load-bearing decision for change failure rate. 13 rows tonight depend on it.
- *The window non-overlap constraint, enforced in the store.* §7 says the caller
  is two agents racing; the 277 cancelled windows are what that looks like.
- *=(change_id, instrument, sha)= as the guard's lookup key*, not
  =(change_id, instrument)=. That is taxonomy class 4 and it is correct.
- *Append-only observations; a withdrawal is a new row.* Kept, with =retracts=
  as an explicit self-FK so the retraction names its subject.
- *No boolean =healthy=; no priority column; no cascade delete; no
  =deployed_by= on CHANGE.* All kept. A build is deployed, not a change.

** Where I would change it

*1. Add =deployment=. §7's biggest gap.* §7 has =ENVIRONMENT= (declared) and
=RUN= (observed) and nothing for an *attempt*. With only those two, #42's
19:43Z event is *unwritable*: a deployment ran, asserted success, placed
nothing, and was retracted 44 seconds later. In §7 that is a =RUN= row that
never appeared — indistinguishable from a deployment nobody started. A failed
deployment needs to be a row, with =outcome ∈ (succeeded, failed, withdrawn,
indeterminate)= and =withdrawn_at= / =withdrawn_reason= as separate facts.

*2. Add =cutover=, distinct from =deployment=.* Under blue/green, placing a
build on the idle colour and pointing the front at it are *different acts with
different risk*, and both rollbacks tonight were cutovers with no deployment at
all. Collapsing them makes a rollback indistinguishable from a redeploy — the
same objection §7 raises against =deployed_by= on CHANGE, applied one level
down. =rolls_back= names what it undid, =to_sha= names what it returned *to*,
and =bad_build_still_resident= records the thing both of tonight's rollbacks
left true and nobody wrote down.

*3. Add =change_authorization=.* §7 has =OBSERVATION= with =instrument= and
=observed_by=, which is right for *evidence* and says nothing about *consent*.
D16 needs =guard=, =guard_verdict=, and =honored= — a boolean whose =false=
means /a guard refused and the step proceeded anyway/. §7 cannot express it, so
a pipeline built on §7 would have recorded #54 as a clean deployment.
=actor_kind= plus =on_behalf_of= is how the =jwalsh= review proxy is told apart
from JW, which the =review:proxy= label already gestures at.

*4. Split =WINDOW.result= from =CHANGE.closure_code=.* §Nomenclature lists one
closure-code enum — =passed/failed/cancelled/expired/backed-out= — covering
both, and §7 puts =closure_code= on CHANGE while WINDOW has =result=. Those are
two enums doing one job. =expired= can only ever be true of a *reservation*;
=backed_out= can only ever be true of a *change*. Two types, no overlap.

*5. Split =forfeited= out of =cancelled=.* Tonight's =cancelled= rows mix "a
person moved it to the 19th" (#71) with "a guard refused at activation" and with
229 rows of a booking loop. Those have different owners and only one is a
signal. §7 does not distinguish them.

*6. Make blast radius rows, and make "undeclared" distinct from "empty".*
=change_app= gives the count *and* which apps. =change_radius_declaration=
exists because #40 and #44 shipped with no =app:*= label, and *"the labeller
said zero"* and *"nobody asked the labeller"* are exactly the two answers
taxonomy class 1 insists must refuse differently. §7 has no radius concept.

*7. =backfill_owed= / =backfilled_at= on CHANGE.* The repo already has a
=change:backfill-owed= label (=7e6785f=, "PERSISTENT"); a label is working
state and this is a debt that must outlive it. Constrained so only an
=emergency= can owe one.

** Named at last: the state with no name

=spec.org= §"The one that has no name yet" describes *deployed, not merged, and
the window is gone* and says a rebuild needs a word for it. The schema offers
=bc_stranded=, a view over =bc_deployed_not_merged= joined to a window whose
result is =expired=, =forfeited= or =cancelled=. *Stranded*: the estate serves
a build trunk does not contain, and the entitlement to do anything about it has
lapsed. #40 was in exactly this state between 00:41Z and its rollback.

** Every boundary condition, as a query

=schema.sql= ends with one view per row of §Boundary conditions —
=bc_deployed_not_merged=, =bc_merged_not_deployed=, =bc_expired_windows=,
=bc_refused=, =bc_refusal_ignored=, =bc_backed_out=, =bc_stale_evidence=,
=bc_stranded= — plus =dora_*= views for the four metrics. That is the grading
criterion §Boundary conditions sets: a rebuild is graded on whether it can
*name* each state. Naming it as a view you can =SELECT= from is the strongest
form of naming available to a schema.
