#+TITLE: 023 — start only: a person says change:start and the process takes over
#+DATE: 2026-09-14

* Hypothesis

Five agents each open one single-line PR and do exactly one further thing:
apply =change:start=. Everything after that is the process: a scheduler on
the mini (=change/scheduler.sh=) that runs =change/driver.sh= one change at
a time, and the forge's workflows. The hypothesis: *a change whose owner
says only start reaches production and settles, and every refusal it meets
is stated on the PR in words its owner can act on without knowing the
pipeline.* Falsified if an owner has to do anything but rebase and say start
again, or if a ticket ends in a state its owner cannot read.

Restarts =experiments/022=, where the same five steps were driven by hand
and the owner's finding was that the tickets were dirty: five tickets
(#97–#101, the other operator's) carried verdicts of a release that never
ended. In this run the driver treats a start on a dirty ticket as paying
that debt (clears the previous release's markers, says so, keeps the
person's own words) rather than asking a person to sweep.

* Setup

- Estate: block 1 (=:9010=) as staging and block 2 (=:9020=) as production,
  redeployed by the driver to the head under release. No windows booked.
- Intent label: =change:start= (label-owners.tsv: supersedes
  =change:requested= + =release=). The scheduler runs the oldest PR number
  first; the label carries no time, so who said start first is not on the
  list.
- Guard 0, first version: the driver refuses a head behind =main= and
  clears every marker including the intent; the owner rebases and says
  start again. Replaced at 23:56Z (see Findings, F1) by the forge's own
  "update branch": a merge of =main= into the branch, nothing of the
  person's rewritten; the driver refuses only a conflict.
- The second identity approves under its standing delegation; the review
  says so. The clearing at end has no identity of its own: it is whoever's
  =gh= is active on the host (jwalsh here, aygp-dr on hydra, the actions bot
  in a workflow). See §Findings.
- Unplanned again: the other operator is live on #97–#101 against the same
  label lock (scenarios D21).
- Logs: =scheduler.log= (the driver's own account), =label-timeline.log=
  (a 20 s poll of every label change on the five PRs).

* Findings

** F1 — refuse-and-rebase livelocks against a moving trunk (23:50–23:54Z)

All five PRs (#104–#108) were refused at guard 0 on their first start: they
had branched from =main= seconds before the driver commit landed. Two
owners rebased and said start again within three minutes, and were refused
again: the other operator landed #101 at 23:49:51 and #99 at 23:53:49, so
=main= moved between the rebase and the driver's look, twice. With a trunk
moving every four minutes and a person's rebase, push, labeller wait and
second start taking three, "rebase and say start again" never converges.
This is the merge queue's reason for existing (#102) arriving as an
incident. Fix taken: the driver asks the forge to bring the branch up to
=main= (=gh pr update-branch=, a merge commit authored by the forge), waits
for the new head and the labeller, and continues; it says so on the PR.
A person is asked only for a conflict. Guard 0 also now runs before the
lock: the first version held the berth for fifteen seconds to discover a
stale head (#104, #105).

** F2 — the tickets were dirty because five releases never ended

Reported as #103. The other operator's loop re-runs the staging
instruments on #97–#101 every ~8 minutes and never ends the release, so
each carries =staging:e2e staging:smoke staging:uat= and the retired
=staging:failed=; five tickets accepted at staging at once is not a state
the estate can be in. The driver's start-sweep makes a start on such a
ticket pay the missing =change:end=; the loop should have paid it.

** F3 — the reaper has no identity

The clearing at end (=settle.sh=, =abort.sh=, =reap.sh=) runs as whichever
=gh= account is active on the host: =jwalsh= here, =aygp-dr= on hydra,
=github-actions[bot]= in a workflow. A machine's removal is indistinguishable
on the forge from a person's, which is D21's finding from the other side.

** F4 — the driver said a person accepted the build; nobody had

The driver recorded the Playwright journey through =change/observe.sh=,
whose comment says "a person used http://127.0.0.1:9010 on build X and
accepted it." Owners of #104 and #105 both asked who the person was: "I
was the only person on this ticket and I did not use anything." The same
defect I filed against the other operator in #103, on my side. Fix:
=gates/uat.sh --pr --env= records its own verdict, the way e2e and smoke
do, and the record says what ran: the journey a person once accepted,
replayed by Playwright; no person looked at this build. label-owners.tsv
names the second writer.

** F5 — two of five conflicted, and that is the one thing left to a person

#107 (checkout) and #108 (pdp) were refused at 00:09Z: the forge could not
merge =main= into them. Both were real: the other operator's #97 changed
the same line of the checkout server, and #106 changed the line next to
#108's in the pdp server. Adjacent one-line changes to the same object
literal are a conflict git cannot resolve. The ticket says "resolve it,
push, and say start again", and the owner does. Nothing else in the run
asked a person for anything after start.

** F6 — operator error, recorded so it is not read as a finding

At 00:01Z the scheduler was restarted to pick up the "heard change:start"
comment while a driver for #105 was mid-run. The new scheduler saw
=change:start= still on #105 (the old driver had not yet reached the lock)
and started a second driver. Both claimed the lock (the record allows the
same PR twice), both ran the staging instruments, and #105 carries every
staging observation twice. The younger was killed at 00:02:11Z. The owner
of #105 reported the duplicates as one of their eleven confusions; that one
is mine. A scheduler must not be replaced while a driver it started is
running, or the driver must take the intent before it does anything else
(it does; the window was the 40 s labeller wait inside guard 0).

Once a change held the berth on a current head, start-to-merge was 68 to
85 seconds. Everything else in the wall clock was waiting: for the berth
(the other operator's loop held it about half the time), and for the owner
to be refused and to rebase.

** F7 — the conflict is the one decision left to a person, and the ticket left them alone with it

#107's owner: "the instruction 'Resolve it' leaves the resolution entirely
to me, with no guidance on whether their color or mine should win... nobody
on the ticket ever commented on that choice." #108's owner resolved wrong
on the first try and pushed it; nothing but the instruments would have
caught it, and the bad head was live for ten seconds before they noticed
themselves. The refusal now names the file and the changes that landed on
it; it still cannot say which intent should win, and should not.

The PIR is the largest unfixed one. It is written for the pipeline's
reviewer and read by the change's owner, and to the owner "Deviation:
deployed outside any change window... the guard was bypassed by not being
invoked" reads as a charge against them. The deviation is the bare-knuckle
mode the estate's operator chose, and the PIR should say whose it is.
Trigger to fix: the first PIR a person other than the operator has to act
on.

* Verdict

The hypothesis holds on its first clause and fails on its second. All five
changes reached production and settled with the owner doing nothing after
start except what a refusal asked: rebase (until F1 removed even that) or
resolve a conflict. No owner ran a gate, deployed, approved, or merged. But
the refusals and the record were not in words an owner could read: four of
five did not know what a marker or an intent was, five of five were told a
person had accepted a build, four of five read the PIR as a list of things
wrong with their change. The process took over. Its language did not.

Not a finding of this run: the other operator's loop held the berth about
half the time and its landings moved =main= every four minutes; that is the
environment D21 describes, and this run happened inside it.
