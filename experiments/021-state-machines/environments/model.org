* What this is

One of five models of the same machine. The primitive is the *pull request*:
every state below is a state OF A PR, except the four rows marked
*ESTATE-GLOBAL*, which are states of the world that a PR merely runs into.

This lens is *environments and the estate*. It asks what each address in the
port map is, what it asserts about a build, who is allowed to reach it, and
what happens to the estate when a change stops being driven. Four other
engineers hold the deployment-lifecycle, gating, protection/override and
end-to-end lenses; where their subject matter appears here it appears only as
far as an environment can see it.

The machine is in three files that merge mechanically:

| file               | rows | what it is |
|--------------------+------+------------|
| =states.tsv=       |   38 | the states, with =kind= and =terminal= |
| =transitions.tsv=  |  113 | the edges, with =weight= and =evidence= |
| =labels.tsv=       |   43 | the label vocabulary, as *semantics* rather than strings |

=transitions.tsv= is normative. The Mermaid below is its readable projection
and deliberately omits the repetitive abandon edge that almost every
non-terminal state carries.

* Framing: three questions an address answers, and one it does not

=spec.org= §The port map makes the port number carry the tier, so that
/"which kind of environment is this?"/ is answerable from the URL alone
(=README.org:521=):

| range     | tier      | promotes | what it means |
|-----------+-----------+----------+---------------|
| 9000–9099 | dev       | no       | worktree blocks, =9000 + 10n=, n ≤ 9. Disposable by definition |
| 9100–9199 | team      | *no*     | real, leased, and a dead end by construction |
| 9200      | protected | yes      | *staging* — the singleton path to production |
| 9210/9220 | protected | yes      | production *blue* and *green* replicas |
| 9230      | protected | yes      | the production *front* — reads the live colour per request; never deployed to |

Three facts are encoded in the number: *what tier*, *whether it may promote*,
and *whether its ports are scarce* — jail-internal =:80= and =8001–8005= are
identical in every environment because each jail has its own IP
(=spec.org:533=). =change/ports.sh:50= enforces the dev/team boundary by
refusing block 10 before writing the row.

*The fourth question has no column anywhere: who may reach it, and whether
reaching it can change anything.* =spec.org:564= §Exposure states the gap
directly — /"An environment registry that names tier and port but not audience
cannot express any of this — it will report an estate as =protected= while the
whole internet can freeze it"/ (=spec.org:648=). This model carries that axis
as two states and one estate-global state:

- =S81_bound_loopback= — the environment IS protected-tier and is reachable by
  nobody: bound to =127.0.0.1=, or in a block outside the range =pf= passes.
  The refusal is immediate and reads exactly like nothing listening
  (=spec.org:545=). Tier says one thing, audience says another, and the
  registry records only the first.
- =S80_unreachable= — the instrument could not reach the address. See
  §Indeterminate is a third value.
- =E93_actuator_exposed= — the control plane is reachable beyond the LAN. This
  changes *no* environment's tier, port or guard; it changes the set of people
  who may press =POST /api/estate/freeze/on=, which halts every deployment and
  carries no authentication (=spec.org:571=). It is an estate state because it
  is a fact about who may move *every* PR.

The rule the model encodes: *tier is a property of the address; audience is a
property of the deployment, and the two can disagree silently.*

* The berth is the only singleton, and it is a lease, not an environment

=spec.org= §Nomenclature: a *berth* is /the singleton lock on the path/, not
/a lock on an environment/. Four positions, and only the last two can be
invalidated by =main= moving (=spec.org:1095=):

| # | position   | model state            | what it holds |
|---+------------+------------------------+---------------|
| 1 | scheduled  | =S20_scheduled=        | a calendar event. Nothing blocks on it |
| 2 | berthed    | =S21_berthed=          | =deploy:staging= — the scarce thing |
| 3 | verified   | =S23_staging_observed= | + the observation stack on this head |
| 4 | cleared    | =S24_cleared=          | + =deploy:production= |

The lock is the *label*, not the workflow concurrency group: a PR that
deployed and is sitting in production verification still holds staging with no
run in flight (=spec.org:1006=). That is exactly why the berth can outlive
everything that would have released it, which is the first unnamed state below.

Capacity, stated as the model sees it: *dev is unbounded-but-ten, team is many,
staging is one.* Guard 1 (=change/queue.sh:47=) is a =gh pr list --label
deploy:staging= query, so its subject is the label set, not the estate. An
environment is never consulted to decide whether the berth is free.

* Which environments may promote, and why the team tier cannot

=environments.tsv:14= is unusually explicit, and it is a statement about
*label namespaces*, not about capability:

#+begin_quote
promotes=no is not a policy toggle. A team environment cannot promote because
nothing downstream of it reads its verdict -- guard 4 names staging:* and
production:* observations and nothing else. Setting it to yes would not grant
the power, it would only make the file lie.
#+end_quote

So the environments lens produces a finding that belongs to it alone:
*there is no =team:= observation namespace.* The 9100 tier cannot promote
because no instrument can write a label about it that anything reads. A
dashboard that shows a team environment green is showing a measurement with no
consumer. In the model, =S12_team_checked_out= has *no edge forward* — only
back to =S00_open= (checked in or the lease expires) and to =S01_requested=,
which starts the real path from scratch. That is a dead end *in the estate*,
and it is deliberate; it is not a dead end in the chain.

A second environment state belongs to this tier and to nothing else:
=S13_team_declared_unactivated=. =environments.tsv= is the map of what is
*named*; =ports.tsv= is the CMDB of what is *running*. A declared, inactive
environment still owns its ports so nothing else is allocated onto them, and
=targets/node/deploy.sh:64= refuses to deploy there with a message that says
which of the two things is true — a reservation, not a typo. *An address can
be occupied by a name.*

* Blue/green occupancy, and the colour that nothing clears

The production address is =:9230=, and it is not a replica. =front.js= reads
=targets/node/live= on *every request* rather than caching it, because the
earlier bastille switch reported its own config file and said blue while green
was serving (=targets/node/front.js:8-12=).

Occupancy has three values per colour, and only two of them have names:

| colour holds | front routes to it | model state | named by the repo |
|--------------+--------------------+-------------+-------------------|
| this build   | yes                | =S31_live=  | yes — "production" |
| this build   | *no*               | =S30_idle_loaded= | *no* |
| a build nobody shipped | no       | =S71_idle_retained= | *no* |

=S30_idle_loaded= is the ordinary, correct, transient case: the build is
installed and answering on the idle replica, reachable from the LAN at
=:9210= or =:9220=, and production is not serving it. =front.js:26-49= makes
it deliberately previewable at the production address via =?__preview=<colour>=
so acceptance can happen before the cutover rather than after.

=S71_idle_retained= is the same occupancy after the change stops. *Nothing
clears an idle colour, ever.* Enumerated:

- =change/abort.sh:78-104= clears labels and posts =failure= to the forge's
  deployment statuses. It never touches a replica.
- =change/settle.sh:244= clears transient labels. It never touches a replica.
- =change/reap.sh= closes windows and releases berths. It never touches a
  replica.
- =targets/node/switch.sh:45= writes the =live= file. It moves the pointer; it
  does not unload anything.
- =targets/node/deploy.sh:85-88= kills the block's ports — but only as the
  first step of deploying *something else into that colour*.

So the only thing that ever clears an idle colour is the next deployment to it,
and the three edges out of =S71_idle_retained= are exactly that, plus the
change closing around it, plus the hazard: =switch.sh:43= refuses to cut over
to a colour that is *not answering*, and checks nothing else. *A retained build
that answers is a valid cutover target.* A later operator cutting "back" to the
idle colour ships a build that was never authorized, and the model gives that
edge a weight rather than pretending it cannot happen.

Stated as the finding: *a closure code describes the change record, and there
is no closure code that describes the estate.* =change:failed= asserts
production never served the build; it asserts nothing about what is loaded and
listening on :9210.

* What an environment ASSERTS versus what it merely SERVES

Serving is a fact about a socket. Asserting is a fact about a *record*, and the
two come apart in both directions.

| address | serves | asserts | how the assertion is made |
|---------+--------+---------+---------------------------|
| dev 9000+10n | a build | *nothing* | no observation namespace exists for it |
| team 9100+   | a build | *nothing* | guard 4 reads =staging:*= and =production:*= only |
| staging 9200 | a build | that this build was measured | =staging:e2e=, =staging:smoke=, =staging:uat= |
| blue/green   | a build | *nothing on its own* | it is a replica; the front decides which is production |
| front 9230   | whatever the live colour serves | that production converged | =production:healthy= over N samples |

Two consequences the model has to be able to hold:

1. *An environment can serve without asserting.* That is =S30_idle_loaded= and
   the whole team tier.
2. *A record can assert without the environment serving.* That is
   =S72_attested_unserved=, and it has three separate mechanisms in this repo:
   - the health check made two requests per route, so during a rolling deploy
     the status code and the build header could come from different replicas
     and the pair passed while the estate was mixed (=spec.org:1256=, since
     fixed by one request carrying both facts);
   - the switch reported the colour by reading its own config file while nginx
     served the other one (=targets/node/switch.sh:54-56=);
   - the deploy reported success, correctly — it *had* deployed — and the
     environment then died with the shell that started it, leaving the worktree
     fully checked out and looking deployed with all six ports refusing
     (=targets/node/deploy.sh:109-118=).

All three produce the same shape: a verdict naming a build the environment is
not running. A model that cannot represent it reads as tidy and is wrong.

* The states with no name

Four, and the repo reached three of them.

** =S70_orphan_berth= — a berth held with no open window, serving nowhere

=deploy:staging= is on the PR, the window is gone, and =:9200= serves something
else or nothing. =change/reap.sh:46-49= names the mechanism without naming the
state:

#+begin_quote
THE BERTH IS THE PART THAT HURTS. A window can lapse having already claimed
staging, and deploy:staging is cleared by settle.sh -- which that change will
never reach. Left alone it blocks every subsequent change with guard 1 while
its own window is gone, which is a deadlock with no declared cause.
#+end_quote

Three facts, and every one of them is readable somewhere different: the label
is on the PR, the window's end is on the calendar, and what :9200 is serving is
in an HTTP header. Nothing joins them. =change/queue.sh= reports =claimed= and
exit 0 for a PR that holds no claim at all (=scenarios.org:241=), so the one
tool that looks like it would answer does not.

** =S71_idle_retained= — an idle colour holding a build nobody shipped

Above. ESTATE-GLOBAL residue that is nonetheless attributable to one PR, which
is why it carries =kind=estate= but sits on the PR's walk.

** =S72_attested_unserved= — reported healthy for a build it is not serving

Above.

** =S74_deployed_unmerged_windowless= — the spec's own unnamed state

=spec.org:2793=: /"Deployed, not merged, and the window is gone. Three facts at
once, and nothing in the vocabulary says it. Reached twice here — once by a
script that reported the opposite."/ Its outgoing edges are the interesting
part: =change/abort.sh= offers =failed= and =backed-out=, and *neither is
true* — =failed= asserts production never served it, =backed-out= asserts an
estate action that has not been performed. The model gives the =failed= edge a
weight and marks it =[H]= in =transitions.tsv=, because no guard in this repo
compares a closure code against what the front is actually serving.

A fifth, smaller one, included because the dev tier deserves the same
treatment: =S11_dev_stale_row= — =ports.tsv= holds a block that nothing is
listening on. =targets/node/deploy.sh:147-151= describes it and fixes it only
for blocks that go through the deployer: /"ports.sh allocates by editing a file
and never asks what is bound, so a block whose process died stayed held forever
and a block started outside the registry was invisible."/

* Indeterminate is a third value

The model has *two* states for it, and no edge from either of them to a pass
that is not a fresh measurement.

- =S80_unreachable= — the instrument could not reach the address. Reached from
  =S20_scheduled= (=gates/preflight.sh:52=, /"exit 4 blocks. An unreadable
  schedule is an unknown, not a yes"/), from =S21_berthed= and
  =S22_staging_serving= (=change/observe.sh:24= refuses to accept a URL that is
  not serving a build), and from =S24_cleared= (guard 6 must *abstain*, exit 4,
  when =deploys(c)= is indeterminate — =spec.org:212=; an empty deploy set
  treated as exempt is how the guard never fired in 484 runs).
- =S81_bound_loopback= — the environment is up, correct, and unreachable by
  construction. This is the case where the third value has a *cause*, and the
  cause is the exposure axis rather than the build.

The edges out of =S80_unreachable= go back to the state that was being measured
(a re-probe that *reaches*), sideways to =S81_bound_loopback= (the cause is
found), or to =S60_failed= (the abstention stands and the change closes).
There is no edge =S80 → S32_prod_observed=. Indeterminate never becomes a pass
without a successful measurement, and it is never a synonym for a failure edge
either: =S72_attested_unserved → S80_unreachable= exists precisely so a
disagreement traced to reachability stops being counted as a bad build.

* Labels as semantics

The prefix is a *namespace*, and each namespace answers a different question
about a different subject. =spec.org:2316= puts it as /the name shape tells you
the kind/; =change/label-owners.tsv= makes ownership machine-readable so a
second writer is an audit failure rather than an argument.

| prefix | subject | semantics | who writes |
|--------+---------+-----------+------------|
| =app:= | the *diff* | observation — an instrument (the labeller) measured the changed files. Derived every push; a hand edit does not survive | labeller |
| =itil:= | the *change* | classification — what KIND of change this is. Exactly one, once classified | labeller derives standard/normal; only a person declares emergency |
| =change:= | the *change record* | state — how far it has got. Overloaded: also holds the retired class labels, recorded rather than renamed because renaming would rewrite history | scheduler, =settle.sh=, =abort.sh=; =change:requested= is the human's ask |
| =deploy:= | the *pipeline* | state — an ACTION IN FLIGHT, never a request. =deploy:staging= is additionally the berth claim guard 1 reads | workflow |
| =staging:= | the *staging environment* | observation — an instrument measured THIS environment and the record names a build | the gate that ran; =staging:uat= is the one exception, where the instrument is a person |
| =production:= | the *production address* | observation — same, at =:9230= | the gate that ran |
| =blocked:= | the *refusal* | state — names the guard that refused, set and cleared by that guard | the refuser |
| =release= / =release:start= | intent / action | the bare word is a person's INTENT and re-fires while it is on; the colon form is the pipeline saying a release has begun, which a person cannot truthfully assert | human / the pipeline |
| =labeler:= | the *labeller* | intent — =labeler:skip= transfers ownership of the derived labels and turns the manifest from derived into asserted, on purpose | human |
| (bare) =freeze=, =emergency= | *the world* | estate — on issue #1, never on a PR | human |
| (bare) =control-plane= | the *diff* | classification — this change alters how every future change is verified | labeller |

*One classification I expect to disagree with a sibling model on, flagged
rather than reconciled:* =labels.tsv= here calls =app:*= an *observation* — an
instrument (the labeller) measured a thing (the diff) and the record is
recomputed on every push, so a human edit does not survive. The argument for
calling it *classification* instead is that it names what the change IS rather
than what anything measured. Neither category in the shared vocabulary fits a
*manifest* cleanly, and the mismatch is worth surfacing at the merge rather
than papering over: =spec.org= §Domain model calls the manifest /derived, never
asserted/, and the five-way split has no cell for "derived".

Two distinctions this lens cares about most:

*The environment-scoped namespaces are the only ones that authorize.* =staging:=
and =production:= exist because guard 4 needs an observation that names both an
environment and a build. There is no =dev:= and no =team:=, and that absence —
not a policy flag — is what makes those tiers unable to promote.

*=freeze= and =emergency= are properties of the world and live on something
that cannot be deployed.* =spec.org:2819=: collapsing the estate namespace into
the change namespace produces /"one label asserting both 'an emergency is in
flight' and 'this change is exempt', so the write that closed the estate
exempted its own carrier"/. =itil:emergency= says /this change is the remedy/;
=emergency= says /something is wrong out there/. Same word, opposite direction.

* Assumptions

1. *One estate, one berth, one production front.* The model is Option B — the
   monorepo with a single berth (=spec.org:1137=). Per-group staging slots are
   explicitly superseded (=spec.org:702=) and are not modelled.
2. *The node target is the estate of record.* =targets/node/= is what runs
   here; =targets/bastille/= exists and its jails are not reachable from a
   GitHub runner (=envs:33-35=). Where the two disagree the node target wins,
   because it deploys the apps that are actually under test
   (=targets/node/deploy.sh:1-12=).
3. *The team tier is declared and unprovisioned.* Three names, three ports, no
   jails behind them (=docs/adr/0002-environment-tiers.org= §What we are not
   building yet). =S12_team_checked_out= therefore describes the leased
   environment the IDP would hand out, not one observed serving. *[H]*
4. *Observation labels are keyed to the head SHA.* A push withdraws them. The
   model collapses the individual =staging:e2e= / =staging:smoke= /
   =staging:uat= writes into one =S23_staging_observed= state; the gating lens
   owns their internal order.
5. *Weights are estimates, not measurements.* See §Running the chain.

* Deliberate exclusions

Owned by the other four lenses, and deliberately thin here:

- *Gate internals.* =lint=, =test=, =e2e=, =gate-selftest=, the fixture
  negative tests, and guard 2's double evaluation appear only as the guard text
  on an edge. The gating lens owns them.
- *Divergence classification.* =inert / pipeline / artifact / hotfix= collapses
  into one =S53_blocked_diverged= state. The protection/override lens owns the
  four-way split and its two thresholds.
- *Approval, review identity and the single-identity problem.* One guard
  clause on =S05_emergency_declared → S24_cleared=.
- *Merge queue, auto-merge, =AUTO_MERGE=0=.* =S40_merged= is reached by one
  edge; who pressed the button is the deployment-lifecycle lens's question.
- *The PIR's content.* =S41_complete → S42_cleaned= carries the ordering
  constraint (the record is posted before the labels are cleared) and nothing
  about what the record says.
- *Jail-internal topology.* =:80= and =8001–8005= are identical in every jail
  and consume none of the 9000-range namespace (=spec.org:533=), so they
  produce no states.
- *Soak.* Proof by use across N later deployments is about a sequence of
  changes, not a state of one PR.

* Running the chain

The contract is that a weighted random walk from =S00_open= reaches a terminal
state. It does: no dead ends, no unreachable states, three terminals
(=S42_cleaned=, =S60_failed=, =S61_backed_out=).

Checked with 20,000 walks, =random.seed(7)=, sampling each outgoing edge in

*The weights are [H].* They are an engineer's estimate of relative likelihood,
not a measurement, and the one thing worth reading off the distribution is
structural rather than numeric: *most of the walk is spent in the
request–block–re-request loop, not on the deploy path.* =S20_scheduled= is
entered about 2.3 times per walk. That is consistent with what this repo
already measured about itself — =research/history/release-strategies.org= reports 10–20
validation runs per shipped change, and =docs/adr/0002= refuses to put DORA on
the dashboard precisely because a forfeited window and a revalidation are
invisible to it. If the weights are ever replaced with measurements, that loop
is the thing to measure first.

Two deliberate shapes in the chain, both stated so a merge does not "fix" them:

- =S02_release_armed= has a *self-loop*. The bare =release= label is a trigger
  that stays on and re-fires (=change/label-owners.tsv:36=). A model without
  the self-loop cannot express why leaving it on is a hazard.
- =S12_team_checked_out= has no forward edge. Team environments cannot promote
  and the model must not invent a path for them.

* Where this model disagrees with a tidier one

Three places, recorded because the tidy version is the tempting one:

1. *A berth is not an environment lock.* Modelling =deploy:staging= as
   "staging is busy" makes =S70_orphan_berth= unrepresentable, because the
   environment is free and the berth is not.
2. *An environment does not have a health.* It has a *build it is serving* and,
   separately, a *record about a build*. Collapsing them makes
   =S72_attested_unserved= unrepresentable, and that state is the one three
   separate defects in this repo produced.
3. *Unreachable is not a failure edge.* Merging =S80_unreachable= into a
   refusal state is how guard 6 came to exempt its way past both of the cases
   it exists for, in two independent implementations (=spec.org:187-224=).
