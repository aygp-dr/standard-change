* Hypothesis

/The whole journey of a change — from =opened= to a closure code — can be
written as a single absorbing Markov chain over states of ONE pull request,
such that a weighted random walk terminates, terminates in a distribution
resembling the night of 2026-09-13/14, and can still name the states that have
no name./

*Falsifiable as stated.* It is refuted if any of these holds:

1. a non-terminal state has no outgoing edge, or a state is unreachable — in
   which case the chain is not a chain;
2. a walk from =opened= fails to terminate inside a step bound — in which case
   it is not absorbing;
3. the terminal distribution cannot be brought near the observed night without
   inventing an edge nothing in the repo supports;
4. the model cannot represent /berth held, window gone/, /deployed, not merged,
   window gone/, or /expired ≠ failed/.

*Result: not refuted.* 52 states, 136 transitions, 10 000 walks, zero
non-terminating walks, and the four unnamed/awkward states are states rather
than footnotes. (3) is the weakest arm and is discussed under §Where the
calibration is soft.

*Success criterion.* =walk.py= runs, validates, and prints the distribution
below from the two TSVs in this directory, with no network and no estate.

#+begin_src sh
python3 experiments/021-state-machines/end-to-end/walk.py
#+end_src

Read-only throughout. Nothing was deployed, merged, booked, labelled, started

* Framing: what the subject is

*The subject of every state in =states.tsv= is one pull request*, with exactly
three exceptions, marked =kind=estate=: =estate_normal=, =estate_frozen= and
=estate_emergency=. Those are properties of the WORLD. They belong to no
change, they are carried on issue #1 rather than on any PR, and =walk.py=
refuses an edge that crosses between the two chains:

#+begin_example
FAIL  edge blocked_freeze -> estate_frozen crosses the change/estate boundary;
      the estate is not a stage of anybody's change
#+end_example

That refusal is the mechanical form of =spec.org= §The distinction that carries
the most weight (=spec.org:2819=): three subjects, three namespaces, and the
estate's belongs on something that cannot be deployed. The PR-side consequence
of a freeze is =blocked_freeze= — a state of the change, caused by a fact about
the world — and the two are different rows.

** Three closure vocabularies, kept apart

| subject | enum | where it lives |
|---------+------+----------------|
| *change* | complete / failed / backed_out / abandoned / superseded | =change:*= labels, =change/abort.sh=, =change/settle.sh= |
| *window* | passed / failed / cancelled / expired | =change/schedule.sh close=, =change/reap.sh= |
| *estate* | normal / frozen / emergency | =freeze= and =emergency= on issue #1 |

The five CHANGE codes are the only terminal states in =states.tsv=. The four
WINDOW codes appear as *non-terminal* states of the PR whose window closed —
=window_expired=, =window_cancelled=, =window_failed=, and (implicitly) a
passed window as the =staging_pass= → =uat_accepted= → =promoted= path. This is
the whole reason the two enums cannot be merged: =spec.org:2787= says an
expired reservation is /not a failure. Its own closure code; the change keeps
everything else/, and in this chain that sentence is a fact about the graph —
=window_expired= has a 0.987 edge back to =authorized_unbooked= and no edge at
all to =change_failed=.

Two of the five change codes are [H]: =change_abandoned= and
=change_superseded= have no writer in the tree. =abort.sh= spells =failed= and
=backed-out= only (=change/abort.sh:120=); /abandoned/ appears as a condition in
=scenarios.org:193= and nowhere as a code. They are in the model because the
brief names them and because a walk needs somewhere to put "the PR was closed
and nobody drove it" — which is otherwise silently folded into =failed=, and
that is defect class 1 wearing a different hat.

* Labels as semantics, not strings

A label's *prefix is a namespace*, and the namespace says who may write it,
what its subject is, and what a reader is entitled to conclude. Five semantic
kinds, in =labels.tsv=:

| semantics | who may write it | subject | what it asserts |
|-----------+------------------+---------+-----------------|
| *intent* | a person | the person | somebody WANTS something. =change:requested=, =release=, =labeler:skip= |
| *state* | one automation | the pipeline | the process HAS REACHED somewhere. =deploy:*=, =blocked:*=, =change:scheduled= |
| *observation* | the instrument that measured it | a BUILD | an instrument MEASURED something, and the record names the build. =staging:e2e=, =production:healthy= |
| *classification* | the labeller, or a person for emergency | the change | what KIND of change this is. =itil:*= |
| *estate* | a person | the WORLD | something is true out there. =freeze=, =emergency= |

The prefixes, as namespaces:

- =app:= — the *manifest*. Derived from the diff, owned by the labeller,
  recomputed every push. A human edit does not survive (scenario L10). Filed
  here as an *observation* whose subject is the diff rather than the estate;
  that is the one place this model stretches the brief's definition, and it is
  deliberate — the alternative is to call it a classification, which would let
  a person assert it, and =spec.org= §Domain model says a manifest that can be
  asserted "is not a manifest — it is a wish".
- =itil:= — the *class*, exactly one. Split out of =change:= on 2026-09-13
  precisely because the rule "a PR has at most one =change:*=" could not be
  stated while one namespace held two kinds of fact.
- =change:= — the *lifecycle*, at most one active, plus the terminal closure
  codes.
- =deploy:= — *actions in flight*, not requests. =deploy:staging= is the berth
  claim guard 1 reads. A person adding it by hand is asserting that guards were
  re-checked at a moment when nobody checked anything.
- =staging:= / =production:= — *observations, per environment*. The name says
  both the instrument and where it pointed. =staging:passed= was retired for
  naming neither.
- =blocked:= — *refusals, one per cause*. Each is set and cleared by the one
  thing that can evaluate its cause.
- =release:= — =release= (bare) is intent; =release:start= is state. The colon
  is doing real work.
- =review:= — =review:proxy= is an observation and is deliberately NOT
  =APPROVED=, so a reader can tell (=change/guard4.sh:85=).
- no prefix — =freeze=, =emergency=, =control-plane=. Two estate facts and one
  classification, and the absence of a prefix is the tell that their subject is
  not the change.

*=itil:emergency= and =emergency= are the pair the vocabulary exists to keep
apart.* Same word, opposite direction: the classification says THIS change is
the remedy and passes the freeze; the estate flag says everything stops and
grants no exemption to whatever PR carries it. In this model they are a state
(=emergency_declared=, kind=intent) and a state of a different chain
(=estate_emergency=, kind=estate), and no edge connects them.

refuses to walk it for termination, and refuses a terminal state inside it.

* The states that have no name

Four, and they are the reason the model is shaped this way rather than tidily.

** =berth_orphaned= — a berth held with no open window

=deploy:staging= still on the PR, the window closed on an exit path
=reap.sh= does not cover, and the build serves nowhere. It blocks *everyone*
via guard 1, with no declared cause. Grounded twice:

- =change/abort.sh:99= — found on #92: a change aborted =failed= that kept
  advertising =deploy:production= afterwards, because the clear-list is written
  by hand and nothing checks it against the labels the pipeline can set.
- =experiments/019-rebuild-001/HELD-OUT.org= H4 — =reap.sh= releases a held
  berth only for windows *it* closes, so a window closed on another path
  strands =deploy:staging= with nothing that will ever release it. The grading
  rule attached to it is the point: /can the rebuild NAME the state?/

The model reaches it from =berthed=, =staging_serving=, =staging_fail=,
=staging_pass= and =preempted=, and leaves it via =reap.sh= (0.765),
=window_expired= (0.20), or a closure. 19.7% of walks visit it at least once.

** =deployed_not_merged_window_gone= — the one =spec.org= says has no word

=spec.org:2793=: /Deployed, not merged, and the window is gone. Three facts at
once, and nothing in the vocabulary says it. Reached twice here — once by a
script that reported the opposite./ It is a state here rather than a comment,
reachable only from =deployed_not_merged=, and it is the only state whose
highest-weight successor is =rolling_back=: with the authorization gone there
is nothing left to finish on.

** =window_expired= — expired is not failed

The load-bearing one for any measurement. 13 windows expired on the night and
not one belongs on either side of the change failure rate
(=experiments/016-dora/notes.org= §3, decision 2). In the graph it has no edge
to =change_failed= at all.

** =authorized_unbooked= — every observation taken, no reservation

=spec.org:2785=. It is also the busiest state in the chain (4.0 expected visits
per walk), because it is where a change lands after *every* recoverable
refusal: a cancelled window, an expired window, a failed window, a released
queue block, a lifted freeze, a released orphaned berth, a withdrawn deployment
record. That concentration is a finding rather than an artefact — it says the
pipeline has exactly one waiting room, and everything that goes wrong short of
a closure code returns there.

* Indeterminate is a third value, and it is three states

Never a synonym for false, so it is never an edge into a refusal. Three
distinct instruments, three states, and each one *blocks* rather than
concludes:

| state | the question | the third answer | where it goes |
|-------+--------------+------------------+---------------|
| =gates_indeterminate= | is the suite green on this head? | =in_progress=, =skipped=, a superseded run, an unreadable forge | poll again (0.858); on bound expiry recorded as *not green* (0.13), never as pass |
| =preflight_abstain= | is there a window, a lock, a freeze? | =exit 4=, the change schedule could not be read | ask again (0.504) or lose the window (0.49); *never* proceed |
| =prod_unconverged= | does the estate serve this build? | HTTP =000=, or a partial sample | re-sample (0.62), or roll back on bound expiry (0.28) |

=gates_indeterminate → gates_red= is the edge that encodes the rule rather than
the reflex: when the poll bound expires the suite is recorded as *not green*.
That is not the same as failed, and the model is honest about conflating them —
see §Deliberate exclusions.

Guard 6's three-valued =deploys(c)= (=sim/pipeline_sim.py:62=) is *not* a state
here. It is a guard on the =settling= and =prod_healthy= edges, and its
=indeterminate= arm is what the =settling → deployed_not_merged= edge
represents. Modelling it as a state would have duplicated the simulator that
already models it properly.

* Compatibility with the existing simulator

This chain is meant to sit beside =sim/pipeline_sim.py= and
=sim/label_sim.py=, not to replace either. The mapping, so the three can be
cross-checked:

| =sim/pipeline_sim.py= =State= | states here |
|-------------------------------+-------------|
| =SUBMITTED= | =opened=, =draft=, =classified=, =class_undefined= |
| =ASSESSING= | =gates_*=, =requested=, =awaiting_review=, =approved=, =authorized_unbooked=, and every =blocked_*= |
| =SCHEDULED= | =scheduled=, =window_due= |
| =IMPLEMENTING= | =berthed= .. =prod_serving= |
| =COMPLETED= | =change_complete= |
| =FAILED= | =change_failed= |
| =CANCELLED= | =window_cancelled= is the WINDOW; =change_abandoned= is the CHANGE |
| =REJECTED= | (unused there; =change_superseded= here) [H] |

The simulator's =CANCELLED= is the one row where the two models disagree about
subjects, and this model takes the =spec.org= §Nomenclature side: /cancelled/ is
a window closure code (=change/schedule.sh:285=), so it cannot also be a change
state. Recorded rather than patched — =pipeline_sim.py= is the source of truth
for scheduling semantics and this is a naming observation about it, not a bug
report.

** Merging with the other four models

All five models in =experiments/021-state-machines/= use the same TSV headers,
so =cat */states.tsv= merges mechanically. Checked 2026-09-14: *239 distinct
state ids across the five, and zero shared*. That is a fact worth stating
plainly — the models do not collide, and they also do not *join*. Nothing in
any of the five says that this model's =berthed= and the deployment model's
staging states are the same state seen at different magnifications. A merged
graph would be five disconnected components, not one machine.

If the five are to be composed rather than filed, the join has to be declared:
a sixth file mapping ids across models, written by whoever owns the composition.
This model is the one that can host it, because it is the only one whose states
span all four facets — but inventing the mapping unilaterally would be asserting
four other people's semantics, so it is named here as the open item rather than
guessed at. [H]

Against =sim/label_sim.py=: its per-PR tuple =(cls, life, draft, release,
booking, berth, prod, verdict, uat, healthy, closed, served, merged, pir,
cleaned)= is the *product* form of this chain. Every state here is a region of
that tuple space; =label_sim= proves what is reachable, this model says how
often. They answer different questions and neither substitutes for the other:
=label_sim='s BFS is a proof within its bound, and a Markov chain is not a proof
of anything.

Two derived rates drive the weights:

- *bookings per change, excluding the runaway.* 326 − 229 = 97 windows over 32
  PRs ≈ 3.0. The model gives 3.8 expected visits to =scheduled= and 1.45 to
  =window_cancelled= (non-runaway cancellations were 48/32 ≈ 1.5). A memoryless
  chain *cannot* reproduce the concentration — D12's runaway is a property of
  one booking loop, not of any change — so the 229 are excluded by construction
  and that exclusion is the single largest modelling decision here.
- *expired share of attempted windows.* 13 expired out of 13 + 19 + 4 = 36
  closed-with-an-outcome windows = 36%. The model gives
  1.13 / (1.13 + 0.49 + ~0.89) ≈ 45%, high but the right order.

| quantity | the night | the model | note |
|----------+-----------+-----------+------|
| changes that reached production and stayed | 17 cutovers, 15 clean | 71.1% complete | 17/21 merged = 81%; the model is lower because it charges abandonment to changes the night's ledger simply does not contain |
| changes returned to a prior state | 2 rollbacks | 8.1% backed_out | 2/21 = 9.5% |
| production attempts needing unplanned remediation | 3 of 18 = 16.7% | =rolling_back= + =prod_withdrawn= = 0.20 visits per walk, over 0.83 =prod_serving= visits ≈ 19% | the closest single comparison in the model, and it lands within 2.5 points without being tuned to |

| the estate emergency | one, 11m58s, blocking everyone | =blocked_freeze= on 7.6% of walks | an estate fact reaching the PR chain only as a refusal, which is the shape the night had |

The headline discrepancy is *abandonment*. The night's ledger has no abandoned
changes in it, because the ledger is built from PIRs and deployment records —
artefacts that only exist for changes somebody drove. A change nobody drove
leaves a window or two and nothing else. So 10.3% is not measured against
anything and is [H]; it is set to the smallest value that keeps every waiting
state honest about the possibility that nobody comes back.

** Where the calibration is soft

- *Bookings per change is high (3.8 vs 3.0).* The loop
  =authorized_unbooked → scheduled → window_cancelled → authorized_unbooked= is
  the only mechanism the chain has for queue churn, so it carries both the real
  re-booking and the model's share of the runaway that was excluded. [H]
- *=prod_serving= at 0.83 visits per walk vs 17 cutovers over 21 merges (0.81)*
  is the one place the model is accidentally exact, and should not be read as
  validation — it is one number agreeing.
- *Nothing calibrates =change_superseded=.* 4.3% is a guess with no
  counterpart in the record. [H]

* Assumptions

1. *One PR, walked alone.* Guard 1 (the berth is a singleton) is a fact about
   the *population*, and this chain has no population — =blocked_queue= carries
   the guard's effect as a probability rather than as a constraint. A model that
   needed guard 1 to be *true* would have to be =tla/StandardChange.tla= or
   =sim/label_sim.py=, both of which hold several PRs at once.
2. *Memoryless.* A change that has already lost three windows is no more likely
   to lose a fourth. The real ledger says otherwise — 229 of 277 cancellations
   belong to three PRs — and that is the largest known way this model is wrong.
3. *The head is stable within a state.* Pushes are modelled as edges back to
   =gates_running= (from =gates_green=, =awaiting_review=,
   =authorized_unbooked=, =blocked_diverged= and =uat_accepted=), which is the
   SHA-binding rule turned into topology: a push voids every verdict, so it
   returns the change to the state before any verdict existed.
4. *Weights are per-change relative likelihoods, not rates.* There is no clock.
   "Mean steps" counts transitions, not minutes, and a step is not a unit of
   time — =sim/pipeline_sim.py= owns simulated time and its 30-minute quantum.
5. *Estate facts are exogenous.* The estate chain does not depend on any PR, and
   no PR changes it. That is true of =freeze= and =emergency= as written
   (=change/label-owners.tsv:53-54=) and false in spirit for an emergency
   *caused* by a change, which this model cannot express.

* Deliberate exclusions

- *Apps, groups, ports, worktrees, blue/green colours.* The four apps are
  fixtures. Deploy groups appear only as the =app:*= rows in =labels.tsv=. The
  port-block model is verified by running apps, and no chain substitutes for
  that.
- *Time.* No slot quantum, no deadlines, no soak. =sim/pipeline_sim.py= has
  those and they are what caps throughput.
- *Concurrency.* No second PR, no lock contention as a mechanism, no eviction
  of a berth holder by an emergency (=label_sim='s =Evict=). The consequences
  appear as weights out of =window_due=; the mechanisms do not appear at all.
- *The distinction between "the suite failed" and "the suite could not be
  judged past its bound".* =gates_indeterminate → gates_red= merges them at the
  moment the bound expires, because the chain has only one "not green" sink.
  =spec.org:2739= says the bound expiring is class 1 — =indeterminate=, said out
  loud — and a faithful model would need a fourth gate state. Recorded as a
  known defect of this model rather than fixed, because fixing it adds a state
  that no transition in the repo distinguishes.
- *Multiple simultaneous windows on one change.* The night had up to 81 on one
  PR. Here a change holds at most one.
- *=control-plane= and the soak.* In =labels.tsv=, absent from the chain.

* Refutation conditions for this model specifically

1. A non-terminal state acquires no outgoing edge, or a state becomes
   unreachable — =walk.py= exits 1 and names it. *Negative-tested*: deleting
   every edge out of =berth_orphaned= produces =NON-TERMINAL DEAD END=; deleting
   every edge into =preempted= produces =UNREACHABLE=; adding
   =change_complete → opened= produces =terminal state ... has 1 outgoing
   edge(s)=; adding =blocked_freeze → estate_frozen= produces the boundary
   refusal. All four exit 1. A checker that cannot fail produces no verdict.
2. Any walk hits the 2000-step cap. None of 10 000 did.
3. An edge is added from a WINDOW closure state directly to =change_failed=.
   That is =expired= folded into =failed= and it refutes §Framing.
4. The estate chain acquires a terminal state, or an edge to or from the change
   chain.
