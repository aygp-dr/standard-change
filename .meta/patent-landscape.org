#+TITLE: Patent landscape check -- "standard rebuild of pipelines"
#+DATE: 2026-09-17

This is due-diligence research, not legal advice. It was produced by reading
patent pages found via web search and Google Patents, not by counsel, and it
has not been checked against a professional prior-art search. Do not rely on
it for a filing or a freedom-to-operate opinion. Every claim below is marked
[E] if a patent page was actually fetched and its number, assignee, and dates
confirmed, or [H] if it rests on a search-result snippet only.

* Summary

The phrase "standard rebuild of pipelines" was read four ways: (1) generating
CI/CD pipeline configuration from a declared profile of answers, (2)
converting a pipeline from one CI system to another, (3) label- or
state-machine-driven deployment gating, and (4) automation of the ITIL
"standard change" class specifically. Angle 1 turned up one close analogue
(Amazon's Live Pipeline Templates family) and one adjacent compliance-artifact
patent (Optum), but nothing assigned to GitLab, GitHub, CircleCI, Harness, or
JFrog on generating-and-refusing pipeline config from a profile [E]/[H] mixed,
see table. Angle 2 turned up almost nothing on point -- no patent was found
covering translation of a pipeline definition from one CI system's format to
another (Jenkins to GitHub Actions or similar); the one hit found
(Red Hat, containerizing a pipeline) is a different mechanism [E]. Angle 3
turned up several patents on data-driven or metric-driven automated
promotion and canary rollout (Kyndryl, Microsoft, Netflix, Citrix) and one
Atlassian automation-rule-builder application that names "label" as a
trigger input, but none of them describe a label on a change record driving
an explicit finite-state promotion machine the way this repo's
=release:start= / =release:end= / =release:skip= labels do [E]. Angle 4,
despite ServiceNow's dominance in ITSM, produced no ServiceNow patent found
and fetched that squarely claims automated, unattended execution of a
pre-authorized "standard change" class; the closest hits are IBM/Kyndryl
change-risk-assessment and template-generation patents, and the 2014 BMC v.
ServiceNow litigation, which covered CMDB/discovery/license-compliance
patents rather than the standard/normal/emergency change classification
itself [E]. Overall: the space is crowded with deployment-automation patents
in general, but nothing found and verified reads on the specific mechanisms
this repo implements as a research artifact.

No patent was found and fetched that describes the specific combination this
repo implements: an exclusive, deadline-bearing berth/lock plus a small
vocabulary of PR labels (=release:start=, =release:end=, =release:skip=)
driving a promotion state machine across named environment tiers. The nearest
analogues split the mechanism apart -- Kyndryl automates the "skip human
review" decision, Microsoft and Netflix/Citrix automate progressive

that labels could plug into.

* What this means for this repo

standard-change is a research and simulation project, not a shipped product,
so none of this is a freedom-to-operate clearance. Read plainly:

- Nothing found and verified reads on =adopt/generate.sh='s specific
  mechanism: enumerate a fixed set of REQUIRED profile keys, check each
  control's precondition against the profile's own vocabulary, and *refuse
  by name* the controls whose precondition fails, rather than emitting them
  weakened or with a TODO. The Amazon Live Pipeline Templates family is the
  closest analogue on angle 1 and it does not have a refusal mechanism --
  it merges inputs into inherited templates. [E] confirmed by fetch.
- Nothing found and verified reads on the =release:start= / =release:end= /
  =release:skip= label-state-machine plus the berth (single exclusive,
  deadline-bearing lease on the path to production) plus the lock. The
  closest analogues (Kyndryl's confidence-scored auto-promotion, Microsoft's
  flight state machine, Netflix/Citrix canary automation) all automate a
  *different* trigger (a computed score or a metric) rather than a small
  fixed vocabulary of labels on the change record itself, and none of them
  couple that to an explicit singleton-lease guard the way spec.org's guard 1
  (=|{x : holds_berth(x)}| <= 1=) does. [E] confirmed by fetch.
- Angle 4 is the most surprising result: for a company as dominant in ITSM
  as ServiceNow, and for a mechanism (standard change = pre-authorized,
  no-approval, per the ITIL definition ServiceNow's own product documentation
  states almost verbatim) that sounds obviously patentable, no ServiceNow
  patent squarely on that mechanism was found or fetched. Either it exists
  under different claim language this search did not hit, or ServiceNow has
  not chosen to patent the standard-change concept itself (plausible, since
  it is largely process/definition rather than a novel technical mechanism,
  and ITIL itself predates ServiceNow). This should be read as "not found,"
  not "does not exist."
- The general deployment-automation and canary-rollout space (Netflix,
  Citrix, Amazon, Microsoft) is crowded, but every patent actually fetched
  in that space keys its automation off a computed signal (confidence score,
  performance metric, risk score) rather than off a human-chosen label
  applied to a change record. That is the one structural difference that
  held up across every fetched patent in this search.
- Net assessment: a clean search, in the sense that due diligence turned up
  no patent that, on being read, looks like it would read on this repo's
  specific mechanisms. That is a real and useful finding for a research
  artifact like this one -- it is not evidence of a thorough
  freedom-to-operate clearance, only evidence that an ordinary diligence pass
  did not find a problem.
